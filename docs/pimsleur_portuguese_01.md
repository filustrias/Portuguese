# Pimsleur — Lesson 01

## Dialogue

An American man approaches a Portuguese woman in the street.

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Com licença, minha senhora. A senhora percebe inglês? | kong lee-SENG-suh, MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh. uh suh-NYAW-ruh pur-SEH-buh eeng-GLAYSH? | Excuse me, madam. Do you understand English? |
| Woman | Não, eu não percebo inglês. Eu falo um pouco de português. | nowng, EH-oo nowng pur-SAY-boo eeng-GLAYSH. EH-oo FAH-loo oong POH-koo duh poor-too-GAYSH. | No, I don't understand English. I speak a little Portuguese. |
| Woman | O senhor é americano? | oo suh-NYOHR eh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo? | Are you American? |
| Man | Eu sou, minha senhora. | EH-oo soh, MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh. | I am, madam. |

## Concepts

**Getting attention.** *Com licença* (<Lat>kong lee-SENG-suh</Lat> - excuse me) is used to get someone's attention or to ask to get past.

**Formal "you".** To a stranger you say *o senhor* (<Lat>oo suh-NYOHR</Lat> - you, to a man; literally "the sir") or *a senhora* (<Lat>uh suh-NYAW-ruh</Lat> - you, to a woman; literally "the lady"). The verb goes in the third person, like "he" or "she": *A senhora percebe?* (<Lat>uh suh-NYAW-ruh pur-SEH-buh?</Lat> - Do you understand?). *Minha senhora* (<Lat>MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh</Lat> - madam) is the classic way to address a woman directly.

**A European word.** In Portugal *perceber* (<Lat>pur-suh-BAYR</Lat> - to understand) is the everyday verb for "understand". In Brazil people prefer *entender*.

**Present tense.** Three verbs so far, each with "I" and "you (formal)":

| Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| eu percebo | EH-oo pur-SAY-boo | I understand |
| a senhora percebe | uh suh-NYAW-ruh pur-SEH-buh | you understand (to a woman) |
| eu falo | EH-oo FAH-loo | I speak |
| o senhor fala | oo suh-NYOHR FAH-luh | you speak (to a man) |
| eu sou | EH-oo soh | I am |
| o senhor é | oo suh-NYOHR eh | you are (to a man) |

**Negation.** *Não* (<Lat>nowng</Lat> - no, not) goes right before the verb: *Eu não percebo.* (<Lat>EH-oo nowng pur-SAY-boo.</Lat> - I don't understand.) An answer often starts with a double *não*: *Não, eu não percebo.* (<Lat>nowng, EH-oo nowng pur-SAY-boo.</Lat> - No, I don't understand.)

**A little.** *Um pouco de* (<Lat>oong POH-koo duh</Lat> - a little, a bit of) goes before the noun: *um pouco de português* (<Lat>oong POH-koo duh poor-too-GAYSH</Lat> - a little Portuguese).

**Yes/no questions.** Word order does not change; only the intonation rises: *O senhor é americano?* (<Lat>oo suh-NYOHR eh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo?</Lat> - Are you American?)

## Exercises

1. Get a woman's attention politely.
2. Ask her if she understands English.
3. Say that you don't understand English.
4. Say that you speak a little Portuguese.
5. Ask a man if he is American.

::: details Answers
1. Com licença, minha senhora.
2. A senhora percebe inglês?
3. Eu não percebo inglês.
4. Eu falo um pouco de português.
5. O senhor é americano?
:::

## Informal version

The same scene as people in Portugal would really say it, still polite with a stranger.

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Olhe, desculpe. Fala inglês? | AW-lyuh, dush-KOOL-puh. FAH-luh eeng-GLAYSH? | Sorry, excuse me. Do you speak English? |
| Woman | Não, não falo inglês. Só arranho um bocadinho de português. | nowng, nowng FAH-loo eeng-GLAYSH. saw uh-RRUH-nyoo oong boo-kuh-DEE-nyoo duh poor-too-GAYSH. | No, I don't speak English. I only get by in a tiny bit of Portuguese. |
| Woman | É americano? | eh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo? | Are you American? |
| Man | Sou, sim. | soh, seeng. | Yes, I am. |

*Minha senhora* sounds ceremonious, almost theatrical, on the street today. To approach a stranger, people say *Desculpe...* (<Lat>dush-KOOL-puh</Lat> - excuse me) or *Olhe, desculpe...* (<Lat>AW-lyuh, dush-KOOL-puh</Lat> - look, sorry); young people among themselves say *Desculpa lá...* (<Lat>dush-KOOL-puh lah</Lat> - sorry, hey).

**Drop the pronoun.** Portuguese leaves out *eu* unless it is contrastive: *Falo um pouco de português.* (<Lat>FAH-loo oong POH-koo duh poor-too-GAYSH.</Lat> - I speak a little Portuguese.) sounds far more natural than repeating *eu*.

**Bocadinho.** The diminutive *um bocadinho* (<Lat>oong boo-kuh-DEE-nyoo</Lat> - a tiny bit) is everywhere and routinely replaces *um pouco*. *Arranhar* (<Lat>uh-rruh-NYAHR</Lat> - literally "to scratch") is the casual way to say you speak a language a little.

**Answering yes.** Repeat the verb instead of only saying *sim*: *É americano?* → *Sou, sim.* (<Lat>soh, seeng.</Lat> - I am, yes.)
