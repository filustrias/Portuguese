# Pimsleur — Lesson 01

## Dialogue

An American man approaches a Portuguese woman in the street.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Com licença, minha senhora. A senhora percebe inglês? | [kõ liˈsẽsɐ ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ ‖ ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ pɨɾˈsɛbɨ ĩˈɡleʃ] | Excuse me, madam. Do you understand English? |
| Woman | Não, eu não percebo inglês. Eu falo um pouco de português. | [nɐ̃w̃ &#124; ew nɐ̃w̃ pɨɾˈsebu ĩˈɡleʃ ‖ ew ˈfalu ũ ˈpoku dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | No, I don't understand English. I speak a little Portuguese. |
| Woman | O senhor é americano? | [u sɨˈɲoɾ ɛ ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu] | Are you American? |
| Man | Eu sou, minha senhora. | [ew so &#124; ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ] | I am, madam. |

## Concepts

**Getting attention.** *Com licença* (<Lat>[kõ liˈsẽsɐ]</Lat> - excuse me) is used to get someone's attention or to ask to get past.

**Formal "you".** To a stranger you say *o senhor* (<Lat>[u sɨˈɲoɾ]</Lat> - you, to a man; literally "the sir") or *a senhora* (<Lat>[ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ]</Lat> - you, to a woman; literally "the lady"). The verb goes in the third person, like "he" or "she": *A senhora percebe?* (<Lat>[ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ pɨɾˈsɛbɨ]</Lat> - Do you understand?). *Minha senhora* (<Lat>[ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ]</Lat> - madam) is the classic way to address a woman directly.

**A European word.** In Portugal *perceber* (<Lat>[pɨɾsɨˈbeɾ]</Lat> - to understand) is the everyday verb for "understand". In Brazil people prefer *entender*.

**Present tense.** Three verbs so far, each with "I" and "you (formal)":

| Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| eu percebo | [ew pɨɾˈsebu] | I understand |
| a senhora percebe | [ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ pɨɾˈsɛbɨ] | you understand (to a woman) |
| eu falo | [ew ˈfalu] | I speak |
| o senhor fala | [u sɨˈɲoɾ ˈfalɐ] | you speak (to a man) |
| eu sou | [ew so] | I am |
| o senhor é | [u sɨˈɲoɾ ɛ] | you are (to a man) |

Notice the vowel change inside the verb: *percebo* has a closed [e], *percebe* an open [ɛ].

**Negation.** *Não* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - no, not) goes right before the verb: *Eu não percebo.* (<Lat>[ew nɐ̃w̃ pɨɾˈsebu]</Lat> - I don't understand.) An answer often starts with a double *não*: *Não, eu não percebo.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ | ew nɐ̃w̃ pɨɾˈsebu]</Lat> - No, I don't understand.)

**A little.** *Um pouco de* (<Lat>[ũ ˈpoku dɨ]</Lat> - a little, a bit of) goes before the noun: *um pouco de português* (<Lat>[ũ ˈpoku dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ]</Lat> - a little Portuguese).

**Yes/no questions.** Word order does not change; only the intonation rises: *O senhor é americano?* (<Lat>[u sɨˈɲoɾ ɛ ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu]</Lat> - Are you American?)

## Exercises

1. Get a woman's attention politely.
2. Ask her if she understands English.
3. Say that you don't understand English.
4. Say that you speak a little Portuguese.
5. Ask a man if he is American.

::: details Answers
1. Com licença, minha senhora.
2. A senhora percebe inglês?
3. Eu não percebo inglês.
4. Eu falo um pouco de português.
5. O senhor é americano?
:::

## Informal version

The same scene as people in Portugal would really say it, still polite with a stranger.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Olhe, desculpe. Fala inglês? | [ˈɔʎɨ &#124; dɨʃˈkuɫpɨ ‖ ˈfalɐ ĩˈɡleʃ] | Sorry, excuse me. Do you speak English? |
| Woman | Não, não falo inglês. Só arranho um bocadinho de português. | [nɐ̃w̃ &#124; nɐ̃w̃ ˈfalu ĩˈɡleʃ ‖ sɔ ɐˈʁɐɲu ũ bukɐˈdiɲu dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | No, I don't speak English. I only get by in a tiny bit of Portuguese. |
| Woman | É americano? | [ɛ ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu] | Are you American? |
| Man | Sou, sim. | [so &#124; sĩ] | Yes, I am. |

*Minha senhora* sounds ceremonious, almost theatrical, on the street today. To approach a stranger, people say *Desculpe...* (<Lat>[dɨʃˈkuɫpɨ]</Lat> - excuse me) or *Olhe, desculpe...* (<Lat>[ˈɔʎɨ | dɨʃˈkuɫpɨ]</Lat> - look, sorry); young people among themselves say *Desculpa lá...* (<Lat>[dɨʃˈkuɫpɐ la]</Lat> - sorry, hey).

**Drop the pronoun.** Portuguese leaves out *eu* unless it is contrastive: *Falo um pouco de português.* (<Lat>[ˈfalu ũ ˈpoku dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ]</Lat> - I speak a little Portuguese.) sounds far more natural than repeating *eu*.

**Bocadinho.** The diminutive *um bocadinho* (<Lat>[ũ bukɐˈdiɲu]</Lat> - a tiny bit) is everywhere and routinely replaces *um pouco*. *Arranhar* (<Lat>[ɐʁɐˈɲaɾ]</Lat> - literally "to scratch") is the casual way to say you speak a language a little.

**Answering yes.** Repeat the verb instead of only saying *sim*: *É americano?* → *Sou, sim.* (<Lat>[so | sĩ]</Lat> - I am, yes.)
