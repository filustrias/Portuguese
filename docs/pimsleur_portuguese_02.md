# Pimsleur — Lesson 02

## Dialogue

A woman calls out to a man she does not know and checks which languages he speaks.

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Woman | Senhor, senhor. | suh-NYOHR, suh-NYOHR. | Sir, sir. |
| Man | Sim, minha senhora. | seeng, MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh. | Yes, madam. |
| Woman | O senhor fala português? | oo suh-NYOHR FAH-luh poor-too-GAYSH? | Do you speak Portuguese? |
| Man | Não, minha senhora. Eu não falo português. | nowng, MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh. EH-oo nowng FAH-loo poor-too-GAYSH. | No, madam. I don't speak Portuguese. |
| Woman | O senhor percebe um pouco de português? | oo suh-NYOHR pur-SEH-buh oong POH-koo duh poor-too-GAYSH? | Do you understand a little Portuguese? |
| Man | Sim, eu percebo um pouco. | seeng, EH-oo pur-SAY-boo oong POH-koo. | Yes, I understand a little. |
| Woman | O senhor fala inglês? | oo suh-NYOHR FAH-luh eeng-GLAYSH? | Do you speak English? |
| Man | Sim, eu falo inglês. | seeng, EH-oo FAH-loo eeng-GLAYSH. | Yes, I speak English. |
| Woman | Ah, o senhor é americano? | ah, oo suh-NYOHR eh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo? | Oh, are you American? |
| Man | Sim, eu sou americano. | seeng, EH-oo soh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo. | Yes, I'm American. |

## Concepts

**Greetings.** *Bom dia* (<Lat>bong DEE-uh</Lat> - good morning) is used until lunchtime. The courtesy question is *Como está?* (<Lat>KOH-moo ish-TAH?</Lat> - How are you?).

**Saying your name.** The verb *chamar-se* (<Lat>shuh-MAHR-suh</Lat> - to be called) takes its pronoun after the verb, joined by a hyphen: *Eu chamo-me John Smith.* (<Lat>EH-oo SHUH-moo-muh John Smith.</Lat> - My name is John Smith.) In Portugal the pronoun normally follows the verb in a plain statement.

**Thank you agrees with the speaker.** A man says *obrigado* (<Lat>oh-bree-GAH-doo</Lat> - thank you), a woman says *obrigada* (<Lat>oh-bree-GAH-duh</Lat> - thank you). It literally means "obliged", so it matches the person speaking, not the person thanked.

**Adjectives of nationality agree too.** *americano* (<Lat>uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo</Lat> - American, man) and *americana* (<Lat>uh-muh-ree-KUH-nuh</Lat> - American, woman).

**Very.** *Muito* (<Lat>MOOYNG-too</Lat> - very) goes before the word it intensifies: *muito bem* (<Lat>MOOYNG-too bayng</Lat> - very well).

**Saying goodbye.** *Adeus* (<Lat>uh-DEH-oosh</Lat> - goodbye) is the formal farewell.

## Exercises

1. Ask a man if he speaks Portuguese.
2. Answer that you understand a little.
3. Say good morning and ask how she is.
4. Say that your name is John Smith.
5. Thank someone, once as a man and once as a woman.

2. Eu percebo um pouco.
3. Bom dia. Como está?
4. Eu chamo-me John Smith.

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Woman | Olhe, desculpe! | AW-lyuh, dush-KOOL-puh! | Excuse me! |
| Man | Diga, com certeza. | DEE-guh, kong sur-TAY-zuh. | Yes, of course, go ahead. |
| Woman | Fala português? | FAH-luh poor-too-GAYSH? | Do you speak Portuguese? |
| Man | Não, não falo português. | nowng, nowng FAH-loo poor-too-GAYSH. | No, I don't speak Portuguese. |
| Woman | Mas percebe alguma coisa? | mush pur-SEH-buh ahl-GOO-muh KOY-zuh? | But do you understand anything? |
| Man | Sim, percebo um bocadinho. | seeng, pur-SAY-boo oong boo-kuh-DEE-nyoo. | Yes, I understand a tiny bit. |
| Woman | E fala inglês? | ee FAH-luh eeng-GLAYSH? | And do you speak English? |
| Man | Falo, falo inglês. | FAH-loo, FAH-loo eeng-GLAYSH. | I do, I speak English. |
| Woman | Ah, é americano? | ah, eh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo? | Oh, are you American? |
| Man | Sou, sou americano. | soh, soh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo. | I am, I'm American. |

**Calling someone.** Repeating *Senhor!* sounds artificial in Portugal. People say *Olhe, por favor!* (<Lat>AW-lyuh, poor fuh-VOHR!</Lat> - excuse me, please!), *Olhe, desculpe!* or simply *Faz favor!* (<Lat>fahsh fuh-VOHR!</Lat> - excuse me!). The answer *Diga* (<Lat>DEE-guh</Lat> - literally "say it") invites the person to go on.

**How are you.** *Como está?* is polite, fine with customers or older people. Among friends the usual question is *Tudo bem?* (<Lat>TOO-doo bayng?</Lat> - all good?), *Como é que é?* (<Lat>KOH-moo eh kuh eh?</Lat> - how's it going?) or *Como é que vão as coisas?* (<Lat>KOH-moo eh kuh vowng ush KOY-zush?</Lat> - how are things?). Typical answers: *Tudo bem, e contigo?* (<Lat>TOO-doo bayng, ee kong-TEE-goo?</Lat> - fine, and you?) or *Vai-se andando.* (<Lat>VY-suh uhn-DUHN-doo.</Lat> - getting by.)

**Your name.** Besides *Chamo-me...*, people very often say *Sou o John* (<Lat>soh oo John</Lat> - I'm John) or *O meu nome é John* (<Lat>oo MEH-oo NOH-muh eh John</Lat> - my name is John). In Portugal first names take the article: *o João* (<Lat>oo zhoo-OWNG</Lat> - João), *a Maria* (<Lat>uh muh-REE-uh</Lat> - Maria).

**Yes, I am.** Answers repeat the main verb: *É americano?* → *Sou, sou.* (<Lat>soh, soh.</Lat> - I am, I am.)
