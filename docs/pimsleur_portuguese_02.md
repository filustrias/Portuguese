# Pimsleur — Lesson 02

## Dialogue

A woman calls out to a man she does not know and checks which languages he speaks.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Woman | Senhor, senhor. | [sɨˈɲoɾ | sɨˈɲoɾ] | Sir, sir. |
| Man | Sim, minha senhora. | [sĩ | ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ] | Yes, madam. |
| Woman | O senhor fala português? | [u sɨˈɲoɾ ˈfalɐ puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | Do you speak Portuguese? |
| Man | Não, minha senhora. Eu não falo português. | [nɐ̃w̃ | ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ ‖ ew nɐ̃w̃ ˈfalu puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | No, madam. I don't speak Portuguese. |
| Woman | O senhor percebe um pouco de português? | [u sɨˈɲoɾ pɨɾˈsɛbɨ ũ ˈpoku dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | Do you understand a little Portuguese? |
| Man | Sim, eu percebo um pouco. | [sĩ | ew pɨɾˈsebu ũ ˈpoku] | Yes, I understand a little. |
| Woman | O senhor fala inglês? | [u sɨˈɲoɾ ˈfalɐ ĩˈɡleʃ] | Do you speak English? |
| Man | Sim, eu falo inglês. | [sĩ | ew ˈfalu ĩˈɡleʃ] | Yes, I speak English. |
| Woman | Ah, o senhor é americano? | [a | u sɨˈɲoɾ ɛ ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu] | Oh, are you American? |
| Man | Sim, eu sou americano. | [sĩ | ew so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu] | Yes, I'm American. |

## Concepts

**Greetings.** *Bom dia* (<Lat>[bõ ˈdiɐ]</Lat> - good morning) is used until lunchtime. The courtesy question is *Como está?* (<Lat>[ˈkomu ɨʃˈta]</Lat> - How are you?).

**Saying your name.** The verb *chamar-se* (<Lat>[ʃɐˈmaɾsɨ]</Lat> - to be called) takes its pronoun after the verb, joined by a hyphen: *Eu chamo-me John Smith.* (<Lat>[ew ˈʃɐmumɨ dʒɔn smiθ]</Lat> - My name is John Smith.) In Portugal the pronoun normally follows the verb in a plain statement.

**Thank you agrees with the speaker.** A man says *obrigado* (<Lat>[ɔbɾiˈɡadu]</Lat> - thank you), a woman says *obrigada* (<Lat>[ɔbɾiˈɡadɐ]</Lat> - thank you). It literally means "obliged", so it matches the person speaking, not the person thanked.

**Adjectives of nationality agree too.** *americano* (<Lat>[ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu]</Lat> - American, man) and *americana* (<Lat>[ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnɐ]</Lat> - American, woman).

**Very.** *Muito* (<Lat>[ˈmũj̃tu]</Lat> - very) goes before the word it intensifies: *muito bem* (<Lat>[ˈmũj̃tu bɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - very well). The *ui* of *muito* is nasal, although there is no *m* or *n* after it.

**Saying goodbye.** *Adeus* (<Lat>[ɐˈdewʃ]</Lat> - goodbye) is the formal farewell.

## Exercises

1. Ask a man if he speaks Portuguese.
2. Answer that you understand a little.
3. Say good morning and ask how she is.
4. Say that your name is John Smith.
5. Thank someone, once as a man and once as a woman.

2. Eu percebo um pouco.
3. Bom dia. Como está?
4. Eu chamo-me John Smith.

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Woman | Olhe, desculpe! | [ˈɔʎɨ | dɨʃˈkuɫpɨ] | Excuse me! |
| Man | Diga, com certeza. | [ˈdiɡɐ | kõ sɨɾˈtezɐ] | Yes, of course, go ahead. |
| Woman | Fala português? | [ˈfalɐ puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | Do you speak Portuguese? |
| Man | Não, não falo português. | [nɐ̃w̃ | nɐ̃w̃ ˈfalu puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | No, I don't speak Portuguese. |
| Woman | Mas percebe alguma coisa? | [mɐʃ pɨɾˈsɛbɨ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ] | But do you understand anything? |
| Man | Sim, percebo um bocadinho. | [sĩ | pɨɾˈsebu ũ bukɐˈdiɲu] | Yes, I understand a tiny bit. |
| Woman | E fala inglês? | [i ˈfalɐ ĩˈɡleʃ] | And do you speak English? |
| Man | Falo, falo inglês. | [ˈfalu | ˈfalu ĩˈɡleʃ] | I do, I speak English. |
| Woman | Ah, é americano? | [a | ɛ ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu] | Oh, are you American? |
| Man | Sou, sou americano. | [so | so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu] | I am, I'm American. |

**Calling someone.** Repeating *Senhor!* sounds artificial in Portugal. People say *Olhe, por favor!* (<Lat>[ˈɔʎɨ | puɾ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - excuse me, please!), *Olhe, desculpe!* or simply *Faz favor!* (<Lat>[faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - excuse me!). The answer *Diga* (<Lat>[ˈdiɡɐ]</Lat> - literally "say it") invites the person to go on.

**How are you.** *Como está?* is polite, fine with customers or older people. Among friends the usual question is *Tudo bem?* (<Lat>[ˈtudu bɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - all good?), *Como é que é?* (<Lat>[ˈkomu ɛ kɨ ɛ]</Lat> - how's it going?) or *Como é que vão as coisas?* (<Lat>[ˈkomu ɛ kɨ vɐ̃w̃ ɐʃ ˈkojzɐʃ]</Lat> - how are things?). Typical answers: *Tudo bem, e contigo?* (<Lat>[ˈtudu bɐ̃j̃ | i kõˈtiɡu]</Lat> - fine, and you?) or *Vai-se andando.* (<Lat>[ˈvajsɨ ɐ̃ˈdɐ̃du]</Lat> - getting by.)

**Your name.** Besides *Chamo-me...*, people very often say *Sou o John* (<Lat>[so u dʒɔn]</Lat> - I'm John) or *O meu nome é John* (<Lat>[u ˈmew ˈnomɨ ɛ dʒɔn]</Lat> - my name is John). In Portugal first names take the article: *o João* (<Lat>[u ʒuˈɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - João), *a Maria* (<Lat>[ɐ mɐˈɾiɐ]</Lat> - Maria).

**Yes, I am.** Answers repeat the main verb: *É americano?* → *Sou, sou.* (<Lat>[so | so]</Lat> - I am, I am.)
