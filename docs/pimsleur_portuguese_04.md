# Pimsleur — Lesson 04

## Dialogue

In the evening, the American man introduces himself to a woman. The twist: she is American too.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Boa noite! Como está? A senhora percebe-me? | [ˈboɐ ˈnojtɨ ‖ ˈkomu ɨʃˈta ‖ ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ pɨɾˈsɛbɨmɨ] | Good evening! How are you? Do you understand me? |
| Man | Eu sou americano, mas eu falo um pouco de português. | [ew so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu &#124; mɐz ew ˈfalu ũ ˈpoku dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ] | I'm American, but I speak a little Portuguese. |
| Man | Eu chamo-me John Smith e sou de Los Angeles. | [ew ˈʃɐmumɨ dʒɔn smiθ i so dɨ luʒ ˈɐ̃ʒɨlɨʃ] | My name is John Smith and I'm from Los Angeles. |
| Woman | Eu não o percebo. Eu sou americana, dos Estados Unidos. Adeus. | [ew nɐ̃w̃ u pɨɾˈsebu ‖ ew so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnɐ &#124; duz ɨʃˈtaduz uˈniduʃ ‖ ɐˈdewʃ] | I don't understand you. I'm American, from the United States. Goodbye. |
| Man | Boa noite, minha senhora. | [ˈboɐ ˈnojtɨ &#124; ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ] | Good night, madam. |

## Concepts

**But.** *Mas* (<Lat>[mɐʃ]</Lat> - but) sets up a contrast: *Eu sou americano, mas falo um pouco de português.* (<Lat>[ew so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu | mɐʃ ˈfalu ũ ˈpoku dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ]</Lat> - I'm American, but I speak a little Portuguese.)

**And.** *E* (<Lat>[i]</Lat> - and) is a plain [i], like English *ee*: *e sou de Los Angeles* (<Lat>[i so dɨ luʒ ˈɐ̃ʒɨlɨʃ]</Lat> - and I'm from Los Angeles).

**Nice to meet you.** *Muito prazer* (<Lat>[ˈmũj̃tu pɾɐˈzeɾ]</Lat> - very nice to meet you).

**Questions with *é que*.** Spoken European Portuguese inserts *é que* (<Lat>[ɛ kɨ]</Lat> - literally "is it that") after the question word:

| Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| De onde é que o senhor é? | [dɨ ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ u sɨˈɲoɾ ɛ] | Where are you from? |
| De onde é que você é? | [dɨ ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ vɔˈse ɛ] | Where are you from? |
| Como é que o senhor está? | [ˈkomu ɛ kɨ u sɨˈɲoɾ ɨʃˈta] | How are you? |
| Onde é que fica o hotel? | [ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ˈfikɐ u ɔˈtɛɫ] | Where is the hotel? |

**Where something is.** *Ficar* (<Lat>[fiˈkaɾ]</Lat> - to be located) gives the fixed location of a building or a street: *Onde é que fica o restaurante?* (<Lat>[ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ˈfikɐ u ʁɨʃtawˈɾɐ̃tɨ]</Lat> - Where is the restaurant?)

**Here and there.** *Aqui* (<Lat>[ɐˈki]</Lat> - here, right by us) and *ali* (<Lat>[ɐˈli]</Lat> - there, in sight).

## Exercises

1. Say that you are American, but that you speak a little Portuguese.
2. Say that your name is John Smith and that you are from Los Angeles.
3. Ask a man where he is from, using *é que*.
4. Ask where the hotel is.
5. Say that the restaurant is there.

::: details Answers
1. Eu sou americano, mas falo um pouco de português.
2. Eu chamo-me John Smith e sou de Los Angeles.
3. De onde é que o senhor é?
4. Onde é que fica o hotel?
5. O restaurante fica ali.
:::

| Man | Sou americano, mas arranho um bocado de português. Sou o John Smith, de Los Angeles. | [so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu &#124; mɐz ɐˈʁɐɲu ũ buˈkadu dɨ puɾtuˈɡeʃ ‖ so u dʒɔn smiθ &#124; dɨ luʒ ˈɐ̃ʒɨlɨʃ] | I'm American, but I get by in a bit of Portuguese. I'm John Smith, from Los Angeles. |
| Woman | Não estou a perceber nada do que está a dizer. Eu também sou americana. Fique bem, boa noite. | [nɐ̃w̃ ɨʃˈto ɐ pɨɾsɨˈbeɾ ˈnadɐ du kɨ ɨʃˈta ɐ diˈzeɾ ‖ ew tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j̃ so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnɐ ‖ ˈfikɨ bɐ̃j̃ &#124; ˈboɐ ˈnojtɨ] | I don't understand a thing you're saying. I'm American too. Take care, good night. |
| Man | Boa noite, então. | [ˈboɐ ˈnojtɨ &#124; ẽˈtɐ̃w̃] | Good night, then. |

***É que* is the key to spoken Portuguese.** It does not sound wordy; it sounds natural and softens the question. *Onde fica?* (<Lat>[ˈõdɨ ˈfikɐ]</Lat> - where is it?) is written or formal; *Onde é que fica?* is what you hear. Likewise *De onde é que és?* (<Lat>[dɨ ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ɛʃ]</Lat> - where are you from?, informal).

**Meeting people.** On being introduced, people commonly say *Muito gosto* (<Lat>[ˈmũj̃tu ˈɡoʃtu]</Lat> - pleased to meet you), *Prazer* (<Lat>[pɾɐˈzeɾ]</Lat> - a pleasure) or *Prazer em conhecer* (<Lat>[pɾɐˈzeɾ ɐ̃j̃ kuɲɨˈseɾ]</Lat> - nice to meet you).

**Softer goodbyes.** A dry *Adeus* can sound final or curt. Everyday alternatives: *Até logo* (<Lat>[ɐˈtɛ ˈlɔɡu]</Lat> - see you later), *Até já* (<Lat>[ɐˈtɛ ʒa]</Lat> - see you in a bit), *Boa noite*, or *Tchau* (<Lat>[tʃaw]</Lat> - bye), very common among young people. *Fique bem* (<Lat>[ˈfikɨ bɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - take care) is a polite closing.
