# Pimsleur — Lesson 04

## Dialogue

In the evening, the American man introduces himself to a woman. The twist: she is American too.

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Boa noite! Como está? A senhora percebe-me? | BOH-uh NOY-tuh! KOH-moo ish-TAH? uh suh-NYAW-ruh pur-SEH-buh-muh? | Good evening! How are you? Do you understand me? |
| Man | Eu sou americano, mas eu falo um pouco de português. | EH-oo soh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo, mush EH-oo FAH-loo oong POH-koo duh poor-too-GAYSH. | I'm American, but I speak a little Portuguese. |
| Man | Eu chamo-me John Smith e sou de Los Angeles. | EH-oo SHUH-moo-muh John Smith ee soh duh Los Angeles. | My name is John Smith and I'm from Los Angeles. |
| Woman | Eu não o percebo. Eu sou americana, dos Estados Unidos. Adeus. | EH-oo nowng oo pur-SAY-boo. EH-oo soh uh-muh-ree-KUH-nuh, dooz ish-TAH-dooz oo-NEE-doosh. uh-DEH-oosh. | I don't understand you. I'm American, from the United States. Goodbye. |
| Man | Boa noite, minha senhora. | BOH-uh NOY-tuh, MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh. | Good night, madam. |

## Concepts

**But.** *Mas* (<Lat>mush</Lat> - but) sets up a contrast: *Eu sou americano, mas falo um pouco de português.* (<Lat>EH-oo soh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo, mush FAH-loo oong POH-koo duh poor-too-GAYSH.</Lat> - I'm American, but I speak a little Portuguese.)

**And.** *E* (<Lat>ee</Lat> - and) sounds like English *ee*: *e sou de Los Angeles* (<Lat>ee soh duh Los Angeles</Lat> - and I'm from Los Angeles).

**Nice to meet you.** *Muito prazer* (<Lat>MOOYNG-too pruh-ZAYR</Lat> - very nice to meet you).

**Questions with *é que*.** Spoken European Portuguese inserts *é que* (<Lat>eh kuh</Lat> - literally "is it that") after the question word:

| Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| De onde é que o senhor é? | duh OHN-duh eh kuh oo suh-NYOHR eh? | Where are you from? |
| De onde é que você é? | duh OHN-duh eh kuh voh-SAY eh? | Where are you from? |
| Como é que o senhor está? | KOH-moo eh kuh oo suh-NYOHR ish-TAH? | How are you? |
| Onde é que fica o hotel? | OHN-duh eh kuh FEE-kuh oo aw-TEHL? | Where is the hotel? |

**Where something is.** *Ficar* (<Lat>fee-KAHR</Lat> - to be located) gives the fixed location of a building or a street: *Onde é que fica o restaurante?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh kuh FEE-kuh oo rrush-tow-RUHN-tuh?</Lat> - Where is the restaurant?)

**Here and there.** *Aqui* (<Lat>uh-KEE</Lat> - here, right by us) and *ali* (<Lat>uh-LEE</Lat> - there, in sight).

## Exercises

1. Say that you are American, but that you speak a little Portuguese.
2. Say that your name is John Smith and that you are from Los Angeles.
3. Ask a man where he is from, using *é que*.
4. Ask where the hotel is.
5. Say that the restaurant is there.

::: details Answers
1. Eu sou americano, mas falo um pouco de português.
2. Eu chamo-me John Smith e sou de Los Angeles.
3. De onde é que o senhor é?
4. Onde é que fica o hotel?
5. O restaurante fica ali.
:::

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Boa noite, tudo bem? Olhe, percebe-me? | BOH-uh NOY-tuh, TOO-doo bayng? AW-lyuh, pur-SEH-buh-muh? | Good evening, all well? Listen, do you understand me? |
| Man | Sou americano, mas arranho um bocado de português. Sou o John Smith, de Los Angeles. | soh uh-muh-ree-KUH-noo, mush uh-RRUH-nyoo oong boo-KAH-doo duh poor-too-GAYSH. soh oo John Smith, duh Los Angeles. | I'm American, but I get by in a bit of Portuguese. I'm John Smith, from Los Angeles. |
| Woman | Não estou a perceber nada do que está a dizer. Eu também sou americana. Fique bem, boa noite. | nowng ish-TOH uh pur-suh-BAYR NAH-duh doo kuh ish-TAH uh dee-ZAYR. EH-oo tuhm-BAYNG soh uh-muh-ree-KUH-nuh. FEE-kuh bayng, BOH-uh NOY-tuh. | I don't understand a thing you're saying. I'm American too. Take care, good night. |
| Man | Boa noite, então. | BOH-uh NOY-tuh, ayng-TOWNG. | Good night, then. |

***É que* is the key to spoken Portuguese.** It does not sound wordy; it sounds natural and softens the question. *Onde fica?* (<Lat>OHN-duh FEE-kuh?</Lat> - where is it?) is written or formal; *Onde é que fica?* is what you hear. Likewise *De onde é que és?* (<Lat>duh OHN-duh eh kuh ehsh?</Lat> - where are you from?, informal).

**Meeting people.** On being introduced, people commonly say *Muito gosto* (<Lat>MOOYNG-too GOHSH-too</Lat> - pleased to meet you), *Prazer* (<Lat>pruh-ZAYR</Lat> - a pleasure) or *Prazer em conhecer* (<Lat>pruh-ZAYR ayng koo-nyuh-SAYR</Lat> - nice to meet you).

**Softer goodbyes.** A dry *Adeus* can sound final or curt. Everyday alternatives: *Até logo* (<Lat>uh-TEH LAW-goo</Lat> - see you later), *Até já* (<Lat>uh-TEH zhah</Lat> - see you in a bit), *Boa noite*, or *Tchau* (<Lat>chow</Lat> - bye), very common among young people. *Fique bem* (<Lat>FEE-kuh bayng</Lat> - take care) is a polite closing.
