# Pimsleur — Lesson 05

## Dialogue

The man asks a woman for directions to a hotel and a restaurant.

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Com licença, minha senhora. | kong lee-SENG-suh, MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh. | Excuse me, madam. |
| Woman | Sim. | seeng. | Yes. |
| Man | Onde é que fica o hotel Portugal? | OHN-duh eh kuh FEE-kuh oo aw-TEHL poor-too-GAHL? | Where is the Hotel Portugal? |
| Woman | O hotel Portugal fica aqui. | oo aw-TEHL poor-too-GAHL FEE-kuh uh-KEE. | The Hotel Portugal is here. |
| Man | E o restaurante do Tavares, onde é que fica? | ee oo rrush-tow-RUHN-tuh doo tuh-VAH-rush, OHN-duh eh kuh FEE-kuh? | And the Tavares restaurant, where is it? |
| Woman | O restaurante fica ali. | oo rrush-tow-RUHN-tuh FEE-kuh uh-LEE. | The restaurant is over there. |
| Man | Onde? | OHN-duh? | Where? |
| Woman | Ali. | uh-LEE. | There. |
| Man | Já percebi. Obrigado, minha senhora. | zhah pur-suh-BEE. oh-bree-GAH-doo, MEE-nyuh suh-NYAW-ruh. | I get it now. Thank you, madam. |

## Concepts

**Please.** *Se faz favor* (<Lat>suh fahsh fuh-VOHR</Lat> - please; literally "if you do the favour") is the most typical form in Portugal, alongside *por favor* (<Lat>poor fuh-VOHR</Lat> - please).

**Got it.** *Já percebi* (<Lat>zhah pur-suh-BEE</Lat> - I've got it, I understand now). *Já* (<Lat>zhah</Lat> - already, now) plus the past tense of *perceber*.

**Knowing vs. understanding.** *Saber* (<Lat>suh-BAYR</Lat> - to know) is about facts: *Eu sei.* (<Lat>EH-oo say.</Lat> - I know.), *Não sei.* (<Lat>nowng say.</Lat> - I don't know.) *Perceber* is about understanding an idea or a language.

***Ficar* vs. *estar*.** Both translate as "to be" for location:

- *ficar* (<Lat>fee-KAHR</Lat>) for things that do not move, like buildings and streets: *Onde é que fica o hotel?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh kuh FEE-kuh oo aw-TEHL?</Lat> - Where is the hotel?);
- *estar* (<Lat>ish-TAHR</Lat>) for people and objects that can move: *Onde é que está a minha mala?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh kuh ish-TAH uh MEE-nyuh MAH-luh?</Lat> - Where is my bag?).

**My, with the article.** *A minha mala* (<Lat>uh MEE-nyuh MAH-luh</Lat> - my bag). In Portugal the possessive almost always comes with the article: *o meu carro* (<Lat>oo MEH-oo KAH-rroo</Lat> - my car), never just *minha mala*, except when addressing someone, as in *minha senhora*.

**To have, and money.** *O senhor tem euros?* (<Lat>oo suh-NYOHR tayng EH-oo-roosh?</Lat> - Do you have euros?), *Tem dólares?* (<Lat>tayng DAW-luh-rush?</Lat> - Do you have dollars?), *Tenho um euro.* (<Lat>TUH-nyoo oong EH-oo-roo.</Lat> - I have one euro.), *alguns euros* (<Lat>ahl-GOONGZ EH-oo-roosh</Lat> - some euros).

**From here.** *A senhora é daqui?* (<Lat>uh suh-NYAW-ruh eh duh-KEE?</Lat> - Are you from here?). *De* + *aqui* = *daqui*.

## Exercises

1. Ask where the Hotel Portugal is, adding *please*.
2. Say that you don't know.
3. Ask where your bag is.
4. Ask a man if he has euros.
5. Ask a woman if she is from here.

1. Onde é que fica o hotel Portugal, se faz favor?
2. Não sei.
3. Onde é que está a minha mala?
4. O senhor tem euros?
5. A senhora é daqui?
:::

***Faz favor.*** *Se faz favor* is often clipped on the street to *faz favor* (<Lat>fahsh fuh-VOHR</Lat>). It works for asking for something, *Um café, se faz favor.* (<Lat>oong kuh-FEH, suh fahsh fuh-VOHR.</Lat> - A coffee, please.), and for serving someone at a counter, *Faz favor, o que vai ser?* (<Lat>fahsh fuh-VOHR, oo kuh vy sayr?</Lat> - Yes please, what will it be?).

**Got it, casually.** *Já percebi* is fine anywhere; you will also hear *Ah, já estou a ver!* (<Lat>ah, zhah ish-TOH uh VAYR!</Lat> - Ah, I see!) and *Ah, já apanhei!* (<Lat>ah, zhah uh-puh-NYAY!</Lat> - Ah, got it!).

**Looking for your things.** When you have lost something, Portuguese uses *pôr* (<Lat>pohr</Lat> - to put) or *andar* (<Lat>uhn-DAHR</Lat> - to walk, to be around): *Onde é que pus a minha mala?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh kuh poosh uh MEE-nyuh MAH-luh?</Lat> - Where did I put my bag?), *Onde é que anda a minha mala?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh kuh UHN-duh uh MEE-nyuh MAH-luh?</Lat> - Where has my bag got to?).
