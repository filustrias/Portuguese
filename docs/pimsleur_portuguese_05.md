# Pimsleur — Lesson 05

## Dialogue

The man asks a woman for directions to a hotel and a restaurant.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Com licença, minha senhora. | [kõ liˈsẽsɐ &#124; ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ] | Excuse me, madam. |
| Woman | Sim. | [sĩ] | Yes. |
| Man | Onde é que fica o hotel Portugal? | [ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ˈfikɐ u ɔˈtɛɫ puɾtuˈɡaɫ] | Where is the Hotel Portugal? |
| Woman | O hotel Portugal fica aqui. | [u ɔˈtɛɫ puɾtuˈɡaɫ ˈfikɐ ɐˈki] | The Hotel Portugal is here. |
| Man | E o restaurante do Tavares, onde é que fica? | [i u ʁɨʃtawˈɾɐ̃tɨ du tɐˈvaɾɨʃ &#124; ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ˈfikɐ] | And the Tavares restaurant, where is it? |
| Woman | O restaurante fica ali. | [u ʁɨʃtawˈɾɐ̃tɨ ˈfikɐ ɐˈli] | The restaurant is over there. |

| Woman | Ali. | [ɐˈli] | There. |
| Man | Já percebi. Obrigado, minha senhora. | [ʒa pɨɾsɨˈbi ‖ ɔbɾiˈɡadu &#124; ˈmiɲɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ] | I get it now. Thank you, madam. |

## Concepts

**Please.** *Se faz favor* (<Lat>[sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - please; literally "if you do the favour") is the most typical form in Portugal, alongside *por favor* (<Lat>[puɾ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - please).

**Got it.** *Já percebi* (<Lat>[ʒa pɨɾsɨˈbi]</Lat> - I've got it, I understand now). *Já* (<Lat>[ʒa]</Lat> - already, now) plus the past tense of *perceber*.

**Knowing vs. understanding.** *Saber* (<Lat>[sɐˈbeɾ]</Lat> - to know) is about facts: *Eu sei.* (<Lat>[ew sɐj]</Lat> - I know.), *Não sei.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ sɐj]</Lat> - I don't know.) *Perceber* is about understanding an idea or a language.

***Ficar* vs. *estar*.** Both translate as "to be" for location:

- *ficar* (<Lat>[fiˈkaɾ]</Lat>) for things that do not move, like buildings and streets: *Onde é que fica o hotel?* (<Lat>[ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ˈfikɐ u ɔˈtɛɫ]</Lat> - Where is the hotel?);
- *estar* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈtaɾ]</Lat>) for people and objects that can move: *Onde é que está a minha mala?* (<Lat>[ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ɨʃˈta ɐ ˈmiɲɐ ˈmalɐ]</Lat> - Where is my bag?).

**My, with the article.** *A minha mala* (<Lat>[ɐ ˈmiɲɐ ˈmalɐ]</Lat> - my bag). In Portugal the possessive almost always comes with the article: *o meu carro* (<Lat>[u ˈmew ˈkaʁu]</Lat> - my car), never just *minha mala*, except when addressing someone, as in *minha senhora*.

**To have, and money.** *O senhor tem euros?* (<Lat>[u sɨˈɲoɾ tɐ̃j̃ ˈewɾuʃ]</Lat> - Do you have euros?), *Tem dólares?* (<Lat>[tɐ̃j̃ ˈdɔlɐɾɨʃ]</Lat> - Do you have dollars?), *Tenho um euro.* (<Lat>[ˈtɐɲu ũ ˈewɾu]</Lat> - I have one euro.), *alguns euros* (<Lat>[aɫˈɡũz ˈewɾuʃ]</Lat> - some euros).

**From here.** *A senhora é daqui?* (<Lat>[ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ ɛ dɐˈki]</Lat> - Are you from here?). *De* + *aqui* = *daqui*.

## Exercises

1. Ask where the Hotel Portugal is, adding *please*.
2. Say that you don't know.
3. Ask where your bag is.
4. Ask a man if he has euros.
5. Ask a woman if she is from here.

::: details Answers
1. Onde é que fica o hotel Portugal, se faz favor?
2. Não sei.
3. Onde é que está a minha mala?
4. O senhor tem euros?
5. A senhora é daqui?
:::

***Faz favor.*** *Se faz favor* is often clipped on the street to *faz favor* (<Lat>[faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat>). It works for asking for something, *Um café, se faz favor.* (<Lat>[ũ kɐˈfɛ | sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - A coffee, please.), and for serving someone at a counter, *Faz favor, o que vai ser?* (<Lat>[faʃ fɐˈvoɾ | u kɨ vaj seɾ]</Lat> - Yes please, what will it be?).

**Got it, casually.** *Já percebi* is fine anywhere; you will also hear *Ah, já estou a ver!* (<Lat>[a | ʒa ɨʃˈto ɐ veɾ]</Lat> - Ah, I see!) and *Ah, já apanhei!* (<Lat>[a | ʒa ɐpɐˈɲɐj]</Lat> - Ah, got it!).

**Looking for your things.** When you have lost something, Portuguese uses *pôr* (<Lat>[poɾ]</Lat> - to put) or *andar* (<Lat>[ɐ̃ˈdaɾ]</Lat> - to walk, to be around): *Onde é que pus a minha mala?* (<Lat>[ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ puz ɐ ˈmiɲɐ ˈmalɐ]</Lat> - Where did I put my bag?), *Onde é que anda a minha mala?* (<Lat>[ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ ˈɐ̃dɐ ɐ ˈmiɲɐ ˈmalɐ]</Lat> - Where has my bag got to?).
