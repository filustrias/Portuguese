# Pimsleur — Lesson 08

## Dialogue

Maria and Carlos pool their money to get something to eat.

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Maria | Olá, Carlos. | aw-LAH, KAHR-loosh. | Hi, Carlos. |
| Carlos | Bom dia, Maria. O que é que você quer? | bong DEE-uh, muh-REE-uh. oo kuh eh kuh voh-SAY kehr? | Good morning, Maria. What would you like? |
| Maria | O que é que eu quero? Quero comer alguma coisa. Quantos euros é que você tem? | oo kuh eh kuh EH-oo KEH-roo? KEH-roo koo-MAYR ahl-GOO-muh KOY-zuh. KWUHN-tooz EH-oo-roosh eh kuh voh-SAY tayng? | What do I want? I want to eat something. How many euros do you have? |
| Carlos | Eu tenho três euros. É muito pouco. E você? | EH-oo TUH-nyoo trayz EH-oo-roosh. eh MOOYNG-too POH-koo. ee voh-SAY? | I have three euros. That's very little. And you? |
| Maria | Tenho quatro... quatro euros. | TUH-nyoo KWAH-troo... KWAH-troo EH-oo-roosh. | I have four... four euros. |
| Carlos | Está bem. Vamos comer. | ish-TAH bayng. VUH-moosh koo-MAYR. | OK. Let's eat. |

## Concepts

**To drink.** *Beber* (<Lat>buh-BAYR</Lat> - to drink): *Quero beber alguma coisa.* (<Lat>KEH-roo buh-BAYR ahl-GOO-muh KOY-zuh.</Lat> - I want to drink something.), *O que é que quer beber?* (<Lat>oo kuh eh kuh kehr buh-BAYR?</Lat> - What would you like to drink?)

**Drinks.** *Uma água mineral* (<Lat>OO-muh AH-gwuh mee-nuh-RAHL</Lat> - a mineral water), *com gelo* (<Lat>kong ZHAY-loo</Lat> - with ice), *sem gelo* (<Lat>sayng ZHAY-loo</Lat> - without ice).

**Numbers agree with the noun.** *Um* and *dois* have feminine forms: *dois euros* (<Lat>doyz EH-oo-roosh</Lat> - two euros) but *duas águas minerais* (<Lat>DOO-uz AH-gwush mee-nuh-RYSH</Lat> - two mineral waters). Words ending in *-al* make the plural in *-ais*: *mineral* → *minerais*.

**Price.** *Quanto é que custa...?* (<Lat>KWUHN-too eh kuh KOOSH-tuh...?</Lat> - How much is...?), *Custa quatro euros.* (<Lat>KOOSH-tuh KWAH-troo EH-oo-roosh.</Lat> - It costs four euros.)

**To the.** *A* (<Lat>ah</Lat> - to) + *o* = *ao* (<Lat>ow</Lat> - to the): *Vamos ao hotel.* (<Lat>VUH-mooz ow aw-TEHL.</Lat> - Let's go to the hotel.), *Vamos ao restaurante.* (<Lat>VUH-mooz ow rrush-tow-RUHN-tuh.</Lat> - Let's go to the restaurant.), *Vamos ao café Nicola.* (<Lat>VUH-mooz ow kuh-FEH nee-KAW-luh.</Lat> - Let's go to Café Nicola.)

**The toilet.** In Portugal it is *a casa de banho* (<Lat>uh KAH-zuh duh BUH-nyoo</Lat> - the toilet, the bathroom): *Onde é que fica a casa de banho, se faz favor?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh kuh FEE-kuh uh KAH-zuh duh BUH-nyoo, suh fahsh fuh-VOHR?</Lat> - Where is the toilet, please?)

**Numbers.** *seis* (<Lat>saysh</Lat> - six), *sete* (<Lat>SEH-tuh</Lat> - seven).

**Or.** *Ou* (<Lat>oh</Lat> - or): *cinco ou seis euros* (<Lat>SEENG-koo oh sayz EH-oo-roosh</Lat> - five or six euros), *com gelo ou sem gelo* (<Lat>kong ZHAY-loo oh sayng ZHAY-loo</Lat> - with or without ice).

## Exercises

1. Say that you want to drink something.
2. Order a mineral water without ice.
3. Ask how much a mineral water costs.
4. Suggest going to the café.
5. Ask where the toilet is, politely.

::: details Answers
1. Quero beber alguma coisa.
2. Uma água mineral sem gelo, se faz favor.
3. Quanto é que custa uma água mineral?
4. Vamos ao café.
5. Onde é que fica a casa de banho, se faz favor?
:::

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Maria | Olá, Carlos! | aw-LAH, KAHR-loosh! | Hi, Carlos! |
| Carlos | Viva, Maria! O que é que te apetece fazer? | VEE-vuh, muh-REE-uh! oo kuh eh kuh tuh uh-puh-TEH-suh fuh-ZAYR? | Hey, Maria! What do you feel like doing? |
| Maria | Olha, comia qualquer coisa. Quanto é que tens aí? | AW-lyuh, koo-MEE-uh kwahl-KEHR KOY-zuh. KWUHN-too eh kuh tayngz uh-EE? | Well, I could eat something. How much have you got? |
| Carlos | Só tenho para aí três euros, não chega para nada. E tu? | saw TUH-nyoo PUH-ruh uh-EE trayz EH-oo-roosh, nowng SHAY-guh PUH-ruh NAH-duh. ee too? | I've only got about three euros, that won't buy anything. And you? |
| Maria | Tenho quatro. | TUH-nyoo KWAH-troo. | I've got four. |
| Carlos | 'Tá bem, juntos já dá para trincar qualquer coisa. Anda daí. | tah bayng, ZHOON-toosh zhah dah PUH-ruh treeng-KAHR kwahl-KEHR KOY-zuh. UHN-duh duh-EE. | OK, together that's enough for a bite. Come on. |

***Apetecer* instead of *querer*.** *O que é que você quer?* can sound abrupt. For food and drink, Portuguese uses *apetecer* (<Lat>uh-puh-tuh-SAYR</Lat> - to feel like): *O que é que te apetece comer?* (<Lat>oo kuh eh kuh tuh uh-puh-TEH-suh koo-MAYR?</Lat> - What do you fancy eating?), *Apetece-te alguma coisa?* (<Lat>uh-puh-TEH-suh-tuh ahl-GOO-muh KOY-zuh?</Lat> - Do you fancy anything?). Waiters ask *O que vai ser para os senhores?* (<Lat>oo kuh vy sayr PUH-ruh oosh suh-NYOH-rush?</Lat> - What will it be for you?) or *O que é que desejava?* (<Lat>oo kuh eh kuh duh-zuh-ZHAH-vuh?</Lat> - What would you like?).

**Ordering water.** Almost nobody says *uma água mineral com gelo* in a café. People ask for *uma água das pedras* (<Lat>OO-muh AH-gwuh duhsh PEH-drush</Lat> - a sparkling water; a well-known brand name) or *uma água lisa* (<Lat>OO-muh AH-gwuh LEE-zuh</Lat> - a still water), and say the temperature: *fresca* (<Lat>FRAYSH-kuh</Lat> - chilled) or *natural* (<Lat>nuh-too-RAHL</Lat> - room temperature). Ice and lemon are an extra: *com uma pedrinha de gelo e limão* (<Lat>kong OO-muh puh-DREE-nyuh duh ZHAY-loo ee lee-MOWNG</Lat> - with a little ice cube and lemon).

**Never *banheiro*.** *Banheiro* is the Brazilian word and gives you away at once. In Portugal: *Onde é a casa de banho, se faz favor?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh uh KAH-zuh duh BUH-nyoo, suh fahsh fuh-VOHR?</Lat> - Where's the toilet, please?) or, more formally, *Onde são os lavabos?* (<Lat>OHN-duh sowng oozh luh-VAH-boosh?</Lat> - Where are the restrooms?).

**Slang.** *Trincar* (<Lat>treeng-KAHR</Lat> - to have a bite, literally "to bite into"); *não chega para nada* (<Lat>nowng SHAY-guh PUH-ruh NAH-duh</Lat> - it's not enough for anything); *comia* (<Lat>koo-MEE-uh</Lat> - I'd eat), the soft past tense used for wishes.
