# Pimsleur — Lesson 09

## Dialogue

João is hungry, Maria is thirsty, and the money is short.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| João | Boa tarde, Maria. Quero comer alguma coisa. E você? | [ˈboɐ ˈtaɾdɨ &#124; mɐˈɾiɐ ‖ ˈkɛɾu kuˈmeɾ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ ‖ i vɔˈse] | Good afternoon, Maria. I want to eat something. And you? |
| Maria | Não, não quero comer nada, mas quero beber alguma coisa. | [nɐ̃w̃ &#124; nɐ̃w̃ ˈkɛɾu kuˈmeɾ ˈnadɐ &#124; mɐʃ ˈkɛɾu bɨˈbeɾ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ] | No, I don't want to eat anything, but I want to drink something. |
| João | Uma água mineral? | [ˈumɐ ˈaɡwɐ minɨˈɾaɫ] | A mineral water? |
| Maria | Sim, se faz favor. | [sĩ &#124; sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ] | Yes, please. |
| João | Sabe quanto é que custa uma água mineral? | [ˈsabɨ ˈkwɐ̃tu ɛ kɨ ˈkuʃtɐ ˈumɐ ˈaɡwɐ minɨˈɾaɫ] | Do you know how much a mineral water costs? |
| Maria | Sim, sei. Custa três euros. Duas águas minerais, seis euros. | [sĩ &#124; sɐj ‖ ˈkuʃtɐ tɾez ˈewɾuʃ ‖ ˈduɐz ˈaɡwɐʒ minɨˈɾajʃ &#124; sɐjz ˈewɾuʃ] | Yes, I do. It's three euros. Two mineral waters, six euros. |
| João | Seis euros? Mas eu não tenho seis euros. Quantos euros é que tem? | [sɐjz ˈewɾuʃ ‖ mɐz ew nɐ̃w̃ ˈtɐɲu sɐjz ˈewɾuʃ ‖ ˈkwɐ̃tuz ˈewɾuz ɛ kɨ tɐ̃j̃] | Six euros? But I don't have six euros. How many euros do you have? |
| Maria | Cinco euros. | [ˈsĩku ˈewɾuʃ] | Five euros. |
| João | Está bem, Maria. Eu tenho um euro. | [ɨʃˈta bɐ̃j̃ &#124; mɐˈɾiɐ ‖ ew ˈtɐɲu ũ ˈewɾu] | OK, Maria. I have one euro. |
| Maria | Muito bem. Vamos ao café. | [ˈmũj̃tu bɐ̃j̃ ‖ ˈvɐmuz aw kɐˈfɛ] | Very good. Let's go to the café. |

## Concepts

**Going to.** *Ir* (<Lat>[iɾ]</Lat> - to go) + infinitive makes the near future: *Eu vou comer.* (<Lat>[ew vo kuˈmeɾ]</Lat> - I'm going to eat.), *Vou pagar.* (<Lat>[vo pɐˈɡaɾ]</Lat> - I'm going to pay.), *Você vai pagar.* (<Lat>[vɔˈse vaj pɐˈɡaɾ]</Lat> - You're going to pay.), *Nós vamos beber.* (<Lat>[nɔʒ ˈvɐmuʒ bɨˈbeɾ]</Lat> - We're going to drink.)

**Where to.** With verbs of movement the question word is *aonde* (<Lat>[ɐˈõdɨ]</Lat> - where to), *a* + *onde*: *Aonde é que vai?* (<Lat>[ɐˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ vaj]</Lat> - Where are you going?), *Aonde?* (<Lat>[ɐˈõdɨ]</Lat> - Where to?)

**Street food.** *Castanhas assadas* (<Lat>[kɐʃˈtɐɲɐz ɐˈsadɐʃ]</Lat> - roasted chestnuts), sold from street carts in Lisbon and Porto in the cold months.

**How much vs. how many.** *Quanto* for things you cannot count, *quantos* for things you can:

| Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Quanto dinheiro é que tem? | [ˈkwɐ̃tu diˈɲɐjɾu ɛ kɨ tɐ̃j̃] | How much money do you have? |
| Quantos euros é que tem? | [ˈkwɐ̃tuz ˈewɾuz ɛ kɨ tɐ̃j̃] | How many euros do you have? |
| Quanto tempo é que tem? | [ˈkwɐ̃tu ˈtẽpu ɛ kɨ tɐ̃j̃] | How much time do you have? |
| Não tenho tempo. | [nɐ̃w̃ ˈtɐɲu ˈtẽpu] | I don't have time. |

**Only.** *Só* (<Lat>[sɔ]</Lat> - only, just): *Só um minuto.* (<Lat>[sɔ ũ miˈnutu]</Lat> - Just a minute.), *Só custa um euro.* (<Lat>[sɔ ˈkuʃtɐ ũ ˈewɾu]</Lat> - It only costs one euro.) Plural: *minutos* (<Lat>[miˈnutuʃ]</Lat> - minutes).

**Numbers.** *oito* (<Lat>[ˈojtu]</Lat> - eight), *nove* (<Lat>[ˈnɔvɨ]</Lat> - nine).

**It's nothing.** *Não é nada.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ɛ ˈnadɐ]</Lat> - It's nothing, it doesn't matter.)

## Exercises

1. Say that you don't want to eat anything, but you want to drink something.
2. Ask a woman if she knows how much a mineral water costs.
3. Say that you are going to pay.
4. Ask a man where he is going.
5. Ask someone to wait just a minute.

::: details Answers
1. Não quero comer nada, mas quero beber alguma coisa.
2. A senhora sabe quanto é que custa uma água mineral?
3. Vou pagar.
4. Aonde é que o senhor vai?
5. Só um minuto.
:::

***Aonde* in practice.** Grammar books require *aonde* with *ir*, but in speech it varies a lot, and you will often hear *Onde é que vais?* (<Lat>[ˈõdɨ ɛ kɨ vajʃ]</Lat> - where are you going?) or *Vais para onde?* (<Lat>[vajʃ ˈpɐɾɐ ˈõdɨ]</Lat> - going where?).

**Just a second.** To ask someone to wait: *Só um bocadinho!* (<Lat>[sɔ ũ bukɐˈdiɲu]</Lat> - just a moment!), *É só um segundo!* (<Lat>[ɛ sɔ ũ sɨˈɡũdu]</Lat> - it's just a second!), *Um minutinho, se faz favor.* (<Lat>[ũ minuˈtiɲu | sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - one little minute, please.)

**Fighting over the bill.** People rarely say a flat *Eu vou pagar*. They say *Deixa estar, pago eu!* (<Lat>[ˈdɐjʃɐ ɨʃˈtaɾ | ˈpaɡu ew]</Lat> - leave it, I'll pay!), *Deixa que eu pago isto.* (<Lat>[ˈdɐjʃɐ kɨ ew ˈpaɡu ˈiʃtu]</Lat> - let me pay for this.) or *Eu convido.* (<Lat>[ew kõˈvidu]</Lat> - my treat.) The other answers *Não, não senhora, pago eu!* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ | nɐ̃w̃ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ | ˈpaɡu ew]</Lat> - no way, I'm paying!) or *Fica para a próxima.* (<Lat>[ˈfikɐ ˈpɐɾɐ ɐ ˈpɾɔsimɐ]</Lat> - next time, then.)

**That's nothing.** *Isso não é nada!* (<Lat>[ˈisu nɐ̃w̃ ɛ ˈnadɐ]</Lat> - that's nothing!) also means something is very cheap.

**Slang.** *Bora* (<Lat>[ˈbɔɾɐ]</Lat> - let's go), short for *vamos embora*; *na boa* (<Lat>[nɐ ˈboɐ]</Lat> - no problem, cool); *meter* (<Lat>[mɨˈteɾ]</Lat> - to put in, here: to chip in).
