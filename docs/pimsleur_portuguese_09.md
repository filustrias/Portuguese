# Pimsleur — Lesson 09

## Dialogue

João is hungry, Maria is thirsty, and the money is short.

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| João | Boa tarde, Maria. Quero comer alguma coisa. E você? | BOH-uh TAHR-duh, muh-REE-uh. KEH-roo koo-MAYR ahl-GOO-muh KOY-zuh. ee voh-SAY? | Good afternoon, Maria. I want to eat something. And you? |
| Maria | Não, não quero comer nada, mas quero beber alguma coisa. | nowng, nowng KEH-roo koo-MAYR NAH-duh, mush KEH-roo buh-BAYR ahl-GOO-muh KOY-zuh. | No, I don't want to eat anything, but I want to drink something. |
| João | Uma água mineral? | OO-muh AH-gwuh mee-nuh-RAHL? | A mineral water? |
| Maria | Sim, se faz favor. | seeng, suh fahsh fuh-VOHR. | Yes, please. |
| João | Sabe quanto é que custa uma água mineral? | SAH-buh KWUHN-too eh kuh KOOSH-tuh OO-muh AH-gwuh mee-nuh-RAHL? | Do you know how much a mineral water costs? |
| Maria | Sim, sei. Custa três euros. Duas águas minerais, seis euros. | seeng, say. KOOSH-tuh trayz EH-oo-roosh. DOO-uz AH-gwush mee-nuh-RYSH, sayz EH-oo-roosh. | Yes, I do. It's three euros. Two mineral waters, six euros. |
| João | Seis euros? Mas eu não tenho seis euros. Quantos euros é que tem? | sayz EH-oo-roosh? mush EH-oo nowng TUH-nyoo sayz EH-oo-roosh. KWUHN-tooz EH-oo-roosh eh kuh tayng? | Six euros? But I don't have six euros. How many euros do you have? |
| Maria | Cinco euros. | SEENG-koo EH-oo-roosh. | Five euros. |
| João | Está bem, Maria. Eu tenho um euro. | ish-TAH bayng, muh-REE-uh. EH-oo TUH-nyoo oong EH-oo-roo. | OK, Maria. I have one euro. |
| Maria | Muito bem. Vamos ao café. | MOOYNG-too bayng. VUH-mooz ow kuh-FEH. | Very good. Let's go to the café. |

## Concepts

**Going to.** *Ir* (<Lat>eer</Lat> - to go) + infinitive makes the near future: *Eu vou comer.* (<Lat>EH-oo voh koo-MAYR.</Lat> - I'm going to eat.), *Vou pagar.* (<Lat>voh puh-GAHR.</Lat> - I'm going to pay.), *Você vai pagar.* (<Lat>voh-SAY vy puh-GAHR.</Lat> - You're going to pay.), *Nós vamos beber.* (<Lat>nawzh VUH-moosh buh-BAYR.</Lat> - We're going to drink.)

**Where to.** With verbs of movement the question word is *aonde* (<Lat>uh-OHN-duh</Lat> - where to), *a* + *onde*: *Aonde é que vai?* (<Lat>uh-OHN-duh eh kuh vy?</Lat> - Where are you going?), *Aonde?* (<Lat>uh-OHN-duh?</Lat> - Where to?)

**Street food.** *Castanhas assadas* (<Lat>kush-TUH-nyuz uh-SAH-dush</Lat> - roasted chestnuts), sold from street carts in Lisbon and Porto in the cold months.

**How much vs. how many.** *Quanto* for things you cannot count, *quantos* for things you can:

| Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Quanto dinheiro é que tem? | KWUHN-too dee-NYAY-roo eh kuh tayng? | How much money do you have? |
| Quantos euros é que tem? | KWUHN-tooz EH-oo-roosh eh kuh tayng? | How many euros do you have? |
| Quanto tempo é que tem? | KWUHN-too TAYNG-poo eh kuh tayng? | How much time do you have? |
| Não tenho tempo. | nowng TUH-nyoo TAYNG-poo. | I don't have time. |

**Only.** *Só* (<Lat>saw</Lat> - only, just): *Só um minuto.* (<Lat>saw oong mee-NOO-too.</Lat> - Just a minute.), *Só custa um euro.* (<Lat>saw KOOSH-tuh oong EH-oo-roo.</Lat> - It only costs one euro.) Plural: *minutos* (<Lat>mee-NOO-toosh</Lat> - minutes).

**Numbers.** *oito* (<Lat>OY-too</Lat> - eight), *nove* (<Lat>NAW-vuh</Lat> - nine).

**It's nothing.** *Não é nada.* (<Lat>nowng eh NAH-duh.</Lat> - It's nothing, it doesn't matter.)

## Exercises

1. Say that you don't want to eat anything, but you want to drink something.
2. Ask a woman if she knows how much a mineral water costs.
3. Say that you are going to pay.
4. Ask a man where he is going.
5. Ask someone to wait just a minute.

::: details Answers
1. Não quero comer nada, mas quero beber alguma coisa.
2. A senhora sabe quanto é que custa uma água mineral?
3. Vou pagar.
4. Aonde é que o senhor vai?
5. Só um minuto.
:::

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | Pronunciation | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| João | Boa tarde, Maria. Ia ali comer qualquer coisa, vens comigo? | BOH-uh TAHR-duh, muh-REE-uh. EE-uh uh-LEE koo-MAYR kwahl-KEHR KOY-zuh, vayngsh koo-MEE-goo? | Afternoon, Maria. I was going to grab something to eat, are you coming? |
| Maria | Olha, fome não tenho nenhuma, mas bebia qualquer coisa fresca. | AW-lyuh, FAW-muh nowng TUH-nyoo nuh-NYOO-muh, mush buh-BEE-uh kwahl-KEHR KOY-zuh FRAYSH-kuh. | Well, I'm not hungry at all, but I'd drink something cold. |
| João | Queres uma água? | KEH-ruz OO-muh AH-gwuh? | Do you want a water? |
| Maria | Pode ser, se faz favor. | PAW-duh sayr, suh fahsh fuh-VOHR. | That'd be fine, please. |
| João | Sabes a quanto é que está a água? | SAH-buz uh KWUHN-too eh kuh ish-TAH uh AH-gwuh? | Do you know how much water is going for? |
| Maria | Sei, deve ser aí uns três euros. Se forem duas, são seis. | say, DEH-vuh sayr uh-EE oongsh trayz EH-oo-roosh. suh FAW-rayng DOO-ush, sowng saysh. | I do, it must be about three euros. If it's two, that's six. |
| João | Pois, mas eu não tenho esse dinheiro todo. Quanto é que tens aí? | poysh, mush EH-oo nowng TUH-nyoo AY-suh dee-NYAY-roo TOH-doo. KWUHN-too eh kuh tayngz uh-EE? | Right, but I don't have all that money. How much have you got? |
| Maria | Tenho cinco euros. | TUH-nyoo SEENG-koo EH-oo-roosh. | I've got five euros. |
| João | Pronto, na boa, eu meto o resto. Bora ao café. | PRONG-too, nuh BOH-uh, EH-oo MEH-too oo RRESH-too. BAW-ruh ow kuh-FEH. | Right, no problem, I'll put in the rest. Let's go to the café. |

***Aonde* in practice.** Grammar books require *aonde* with *ir*, but in speech it varies a lot, and you will often hear *Onde é que vais?* (<Lat>OHN-duh eh kuh vysh?</Lat> - where are you going?) or *Vais para onde?* (<Lat>vysh PUH-ruh OHN-duh?</Lat> - going where?).

**Just a second.** To ask someone to wait: *Só um bocadinho!* (<Lat>saw oong boo-kuh-DEE-nyoo!</Lat> - just a moment!), *É só um segundo!* (<Lat>eh saw oong suh-GOON-doo!</Lat> - it's just a second!), *Um minutinho, se faz favor.* (<Lat>oong mee-noo-TEE-nyoo, suh fahsh fuh-VOHR.</Lat> - one little minute, please.)

**Fighting over the bill.** People rarely say a flat *Eu vou pagar*. They say *Deixa estar, pago eu!* (<Lat>DAY-shuh ish-TAHR, PAH-goo EH-oo!</Lat> - leave it, I'll pay!), *Deixa que eu pago isto.* (<Lat>DAY-shuh kuh EH-oo PAH-goo EESH-too.</Lat> - let me pay for this.) or *Eu convido.* (<Lat>EH-oo kong-VEE-doo.</Lat> - my treat.) The other answers *Não, não senhora, pago eu!* (<Lat>nowng, nowng suh-NYAW-ruh, PAH-goo EH-oo!</Lat> - no way, I'm paying!) or *Fica para a próxima.* (<Lat>FEE-kuh PUH-ruh uh PRAW-see-muh.</Lat> - next time, then.)

**That's nothing.** *Isso não é nada!* (<Lat>EE-soo nowng eh NAH-duh!</Lat> - that's nothing!) also means something is very cheap.

**Slang.** *Bora* (<Lat>BAW-ruh</Lat> - let's go), short for *vamos embora*; *na boa* (<Lat>nuh BOH-uh</Lat> - no problem, cool); *meter* (<Lat>muh-TAYR</Lat> - to put in, here: to chip in).
