# Pimsleur — Lesson 11

## Dialogue

A customer orders at the counter of a pastry shop.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Customer | Quero um pastel de nata, se faz favor. A senhora tem água mineral? | [ˈkɛɾu ũ pɐʃˈtɛɫ dɨ ˈnatɐ &#124; sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ ‖ ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ tɐ̃j̃ ˈaɡwɐ minɨˈɾaɫ] | I'd like a custard tart, please. Do you have mineral water? |
| Waitress | Sim, tenho. O senhor quer uma? | [sĩ &#124; ˈtɐɲu ‖ u sɨˈɲoɾ kɛɾ ˈumɐ] | Yes, I do. Would you like one? |
| Customer | Está gelada? | [ɨʃˈta ʒɨˈladɐ] | Is it ice-cold? |
| Waitress | Não, não está gelada. | [nɐ̃w̃ &#124; nɐ̃w̃ ɨʃˈta ʒɨˈladɐ] | No, it isn't ice-cold. |
| Customer | A senhora tem gelo? | [ɐ sɨˈɲɔɾɐ tɐ̃j̃ ˈʒelu] | Do you have ice? |
| Waitress | Sim, tenho gelo e muito gelado. | [sĩ &#124; ˈtɐɲu ˈʒelu i ˈmũj̃tu ʒɨˈladu] | Yes, I have ice, and lots of ice cream. |

## Concepts

**Ice and ice cream.** *Gelo* (<Lat>[ˈʒelu]</Lat> - ice) and *gelado* (<Lat>[ʒɨˈladu]</Lat> - ice cream) are different words. The adjective *gelado* / *gelada* (<Lat>[ʒɨˈladɐ]</Lat> - ice-cold) agrees with the noun: *a água está gelada*.

**To buy.** *Comprar* (<Lat>[kõˈpɾaɾ]</Lat> - to buy) with *ir* for the near future: *Vou comprar alguma coisa.* (<Lat>[vo kõˈpɾaɾ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ]</Lat> - I'm going to buy something.), *O que é que vai comprar?* (<Lat>[u kɨ ɛ kɨ vaj kõˈpɾaɾ]</Lat> - What are you going to buy?), *Não sei o que é que vou comprar.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ sɐj u kɨ ɛ kɨ vo kõˈpɾaɾ]</Lat> - I don't know what I'm going to buy.)

**Can.** *Poder* (<Lat>[puˈdeɾ]</Lat> - to be able to, may) is followed by the infinitive: *Posso comprar muitas coisas.* (<Lat>[ˈpɔsu kõˈpɾaɾ ˈmũj̃tɐʃ ˈkojzɐʃ]</Lat> - I can buy lots of things.), *Pode comprar.* (<Lat>[ˈpɔdɨ kõˈpɾaɾ]</Lat> - You can buy.), *Não pode.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ˈpɔdɨ]</Lat> - You can't.), *Posso pagar.* (<Lat>[ˈpɔsu pɐˈɡaɾ]</Lat> - I can pay.)

**There is, there are.** *Há* (<Lat>[a]</Lat> - there is, there are) never changes: *Há coisas boas para comer em Portugal.* (<Lat>[a ˈkojzɐʒ ˈboɐʃ ˈpɐɾɐ kuˈmeɾ ɐ̃j̃ puɾtuˈɡaɫ]</Lat> - There are good things to eat in Portugal.), *Há dois euros aqui.* (<Lat>[a dojz ˈewɾuz ɐˈki]</Lat> - There are two euros here.), *Quantos euros há?* (<Lat>[ˈkwɐ̃tuz ˈewɾuz a]</Lat> - How many euros are there?)

**Good things.** *Bom* becomes *boa* (<Lat>[ˈboɐ]</Lat> - good) with a feminine noun, and *boas* in the plural: *coisas muito boas* (<Lat>[ˈkojzɐʒ ˈmũj̃tu ˈboɐʃ]</Lat> - very good things). *Para* + infinitive gives the purpose: *para comer* (<Lat>[ˈpɐɾɐ kuˈmeɾ]</Lat> - to eat).

**Do you like Portugal?** *Gosta de Portugal?* (<Lat>[ˈɡɔʃtɐ dɨ puɾtuˈɡaɫ]</Lat> - Do you like Portugal?) → *Gosto muito de Portugal.* (<Lat>[ˈɡɔʃtu ˈmũj̃tu dɨ puɾtuˈɡaɫ]</Lat> - I like Portugal a lot.)

**Pois é.** *Pois é* (<Lat>[pojz ɛ]</Lat> - that's right, exactly) agrees with something just said or remembered. You will hear it constantly: *Ah, pois é, o senhor vai beber água gelada.* (<Lat>[a &#124; pojz ɛ &#124; u sɨˈɲoɾ vaj bɨˈbeɾ ˈaɡwɐ ʒɨˈladɐ]</Lat> - Ah, that's right, you're going to drink ice-cold water.)

**Numbers.** *doze* (<Lat>[ˈdozɨ]</Lat> - twelve), *treze* (<Lat>[ˈtɾezɨ]</Lat> - thirteen).

2. Ask if the water is ice-cold.
3. Say that you are going to buy something.
4. Say that there are good things to eat in Portugal.
5. Ask someone (formal) if they like Portugal.

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Customer | Olhe, boa tarde! Era um pastel de nata e uma água, se faz favor. | [ˈɔʎɨ &#124; ˈboɐ ˈtaɾdɨ ‖ ˈɛɾɐ ũ pɐʃˈtɛɫ dɨ ˈnatɐ i ˈumɐ ˈaɡwɐ &#124; sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ] | Good afternoon! A custard tart and a water, please. |
| Waitress | Com certeza. Quer água natural ou fresca? | [kõ sɨɾˈtezɐ ‖ kɛɾ ˈaɡwɐ nɐtuˈɾaɫ o ˈfɾeʃkɐ] | Of course. Room temperature or chilled? |
| Customer | Bem fresca, se tiver. | [bɐ̃j̃ ˈfɾeʃkɐ &#124; sɨ tiˈveɾ] | Nice and cold, if you have it. |
| Waitress | Olhe que no frigorífico já não tenho nenhuma bem fria... | [ˈɔʎɨ kɨ nu fɾiɡuˈɾifiku ʒa nɐ̃w̃ ˈtɐɲu nɨˈɲumɐ bɐ̃j̃ ˈfɾiɐ] | I'm afraid there's none really cold left in the fridge... |
| Customer | Ah, não faz mal. Se puder deitar aí umas pedras de gelo num copo, agradecia! | [a &#124; nɐ̃w̃ faʒ maɫ ‖ sɨ puˈdeɾ dɐjˈtaɾ ɐˈi ˈumɐʃ ˈpɛdɾɐʒ dɨ ˈʒelu nũ ˈkɔpu &#124; ɐɡɾɐdɨˈsiɐ] | Ah, never mind. If you could put a few ice cubes in a glass, I'd be grateful! |
| Waitress | Sai já! | [saj ʒa] | Coming right up! |

**Ordering politely.** At a counter, *Quero...* sounds pushy. The polite form is the imperfect tense: *Era um pastel de nata, se faz favor.* (<Lat>[ˈɛɾɐ ũ pɐʃˈtɛɫ dɨ ˈnatɐ &#124; sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - I'll have a custard tart, please.) or *Queria um pastel de nata e uma água das pedras.* (<Lat>[kɨˈɾiɐ ũ pɐʃˈtɛɫ dɨ ˈnatɐ i ˈumɐ ˈaɡwɐ dɐʃ ˈpɛdɾɐʃ]</Lat> - I'd like a custard tart and a sparkling water.)

**Fresca, not gelada.** The everyday question is whether a drink is *fresca* (<Lat>[ˈfɾeʃkɐ]</Lat> - chilled): *Tem água fresca?* (<Lat>[tɐ̃j̃ ˈaɡwɐ ˈfɾeʃkɐ]</Lat> - Do you have cold water?). *Gelada* sounds extreme, almost frozen. Water is ordered as *água sem gás* (<Lat>[ˈaɡwɐ sɐ̃j̃ ɡaʃ]</Lat> - still water), *água lisa* or *água natural*, or *água com gás* (<Lat>[ˈaɡwɐ kõ ɡaʃ]</Lat> - sparkling water), often by the brand name *água das pedras*.

**Stones of ice.** Ice cubes are *pedras de gelo* (<Lat>[ˈpɛdɾɐʒ dɨ ˈʒelu]</Lat> - literally "stones of ice"): *Podia deitar uma pedrinha de gelo no copo, se faz favor?* (<Lat>[puˈdiɐ dɐjˈtaɾ ˈumɐ pɨˈdɾiɲɐ dɨ ˈʒelu nu ˈkɔpu &#124; sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - Could you put an ice cube in the glass, please?) *Deitar* (<Lat>[dɐjˈtaɾ]</Lat> - to pour, to put in) is the usual verb.

**Don't say *a senhora* every time.** In a shop, people drop it and use the verb alone: *Tem água fresca?* rather than *A senhora tem água fresca?*

**Slang.** *Sai já!* (<Lat>[saj ʒa]</Lat> - coming right up!, literally "it leaves now") is what a waiter says when your order is on its way; *não faz mal* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ faʒ maɫ]</Lat> - never mind, no problem).
