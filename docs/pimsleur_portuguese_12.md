# Pimsleur — Lesson 12

## Dialogue

João and Luísa have a coffee together and argue over who pays.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| João | Gosta de galão, Luísa? | [ˈɡɔʃtɐ dɨ ɡɐˈlɐ̃w̃ &#124; luˈizɐ] | Do you like your galão, Luísa? |
| Luísa | Sim, João. Está muito bom e vou pagar. | [sĩ &#124; ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ ‖ ɨʃˈta ˈmũj̃tu bõ i vo pɐˈɡaɾ] | Yes, João. It's very good, and I'm going to pay. |
| João | Não, não pode pagar. Eu vou pagar. | [nɐ̃w̃ &#124; nɐ̃w̃ ˈpɔdɨ pɐˈɡaɾ ‖ ew vo pɐˈɡaɾ] | No, you can't pay. I'm going to pay. |
| Luísa | Eu quero pagar. | [ew ˈkɛɾu pɐˈɡaɾ] | I want to pay. |
| João | Está bem, pode pagar. E muito obrigado. | [ɨʃˈta bɐ̃j̃ &#124; ˈpɔdɨ pɐˈɡaɾ ‖ i ˈmũj̃tu ɔbɾiˈɡadu] | All right, you can pay. And thank you very much. |
| Luísa | De nada, João. Mas amanhã eu vou pagar! | [dɨ ˈnadɐ &#124; ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ ‖ mɐz ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃ ew vo pɐˈɡaɾ] | You're welcome, João. But tomorrow I'm going to pay! |

## Concepts

**Um galão.** *Um galão* (<Lat>[ũ ɡɐˈlɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - a milky coffee in a glass) is about a quarter espresso and three quarters hot milk, served in a tall glass with a long spoon. A breakfast and afternoon favourite.

**Present tense, I and you.** *Eu compro* (<Lat>[ew ˈkõpɾu]</Lat> - I buy), *você compra* (<Lat>[vɔˈse ˈkõpɾɐ]</Lat> - you buy); *eu pago* (<Lat>[ew ˈpaɡu]</Lat> - I pay), *você paga* (<Lat>[vɔˈse ˈpaɡɐ]</Lat> - you pay).

**If you like.** *Se quiser* (<Lat>[sɨ kiˈzeɾ]</Lat> - if you want, if you like) uses a special verb form for future possibilities: *Pode pagar se quiser.* (<Lat>[ˈpɔdɨ pɐˈɡaɾ sɨ kiˈzeɾ]</Lat> - You can pay if you like.), *Você compra se quiser.* (<Lat>[vɔˈse ˈkõpɾɐ sɨ kiˈzeɾ]</Lat> - You buy, if you want.) Learn it as a fixed phrase for now.

**Today and tomorrow.** *Hoje* (<Lat>[ˈoʒɨ]</Lat> - today): *hoje não* (<Lat>[ˈoʒɨ nɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - not today). *Amanhã* (<Lat>[ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃]</Lat> - tomorrow): *amanhã há tempo* (<Lat>[ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃ a ˈtẽpu]</Lat> - there's time tomorrow).

**At what time?** *A que horas?* (<Lat>[ɐ kɨ ˈɔɾɐʃ]</Lat> - at what time?): *A que horas é que quer comer?* (<Lat>[ɐ kɨ ˈɔɾɐz ɛ kɨ kɛɾ kuˈmeɾ]</Lat> - What time do you want to eat?) Half past is *e meia* (<Lat>[i ˈmɐjɐ]</Lat> - and a half): *às oito e meia* (<Lat>[aʒ ˈojtu i ˈmɐjɐ]</Lat> - at half past eight), *às sete e meia* (<Lat>[aʃ ˈsɛtɨ i ˈmɐjɐ]</Lat> - at half past seven), *às nove e meia* (<Lat>[aʒ ˈnɔvɨ i ˈmɐjɐ]</Lat> - at half past nine).

**Numbers.** *catorze* (<Lat>[kɐˈtoɾzɨ]</Lat> - fourteen; also spelt *quatorze*), *quinze* (<Lat>[ˈkĩzɨ]</Lat> - fifteen).

## Exercises

1. Ask Luísa (with *você*) if she likes the galão.
2. Say that you are going to pay.
3. Tell someone (formal) that they can pay if they like.
4. Ask at what time she wants to eat.
5. Say *at half past eight*.

::: details Answers
1. Gosta de galão, Luísa?
2. Vou pagar.
3. Pode pagar se quiser.
4. A que horas é que quer comer?
5. Às oito e meia.
:::

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| João | Então, Luísa, o galão está bom? | [ẽˈtɐ̃w̃ &#124; luˈizɐ &#124; u ɡɐˈlɐ̃w̃ ɨʃˈta bõ] | So, Luísa, is the galão good? |
| Luísa | Está ótimo! Deixa que eu pago a despesa. | [ɨʃˈta ˈɔtimu ‖ ˈdɐjʃɐ kɨ ew ˈpaɡu ɐ dɨʃˈpezɐ] | It's great! Let me get the bill. |
| João | Nem pensar, hoje pago eu! | [nɐ̃j̃ pẽˈsaɾ &#124; ˈoʒɨ ˈpaɡu ew] | No way, today I'm paying! |
| Luísa | Mas eu faço mesmo questão de pagar hoje! | [mɐz ew ˈfasu ˈmeʒmu kɨʃˈtɐ̃w̃ dɨ pɐˈɡaɾ ˈoʒɨ] | But I really insist on paying today! |
| João | Pronto, está bem, não se fala mais nisso. Obrigado! | [ˈpɾõtu &#124; ɨʃˈta bɐ̃j̃ &#124; nɐ̃w̃ sɨ ˈfalɐ majʒ ˈnisu ‖ ɔbɾiˈɡadu] | Fine, all right, say no more. Thanks! |
| Luísa | De nada, amigo! Mas olha que amanhã ficas a ver navios, que pago eu outra vez! | [dɨ ˈnadɐ &#124; ɐˈmiɡu ‖ mɐz ˈɔʎɐ kɨ ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃ ˈfikɐz ɐ veɾ nɐˈviuʃ &#124; kɨ ˈpaɡu ew ˈotɾɐ veʃ] | You're welcome, my friend! But mind you, tomorrow you'll miss out again, because I'm paying! |

**The battle for the bill.** Insisting on paying for coffee is a deeply rooted ritual. People don't trade *eu vou pagar* back and forth; they use set phrases: *Deixa estar, pago eu!* (<Lat>[ˈdɐjʃɐ ɨʃˈtaɾ &#124; ˈpaɡu ew]</Lat> - leave it, I'm paying!), *Nem penses, hoje fica por minha conta!* (<Lat>[nɐ̃j̃ ˈpẽsɨʃ &#124; ˈoʒɨ ˈfikɐ puɾ ˈmiɲɐ ˈkõtɐ]</Lat> - don't even think about it, today it's on me!), *Olha que hoje pago eu, amanhã pagas tu.* (<Lat>[ˈɔʎɐ kɨ ˈoʒɨ ˈpaɡu ew &#124; ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃ ˈpaɡɐʃ tu]</Lat> - today I pay, tomorrow you pay.)

**Pago eu.** To stress who does something, spoken Portuguese puts the pronoun after the verb: *pago eu* (<Lat>[ˈpaɡu ew]</Lat> - I'll pay, me), *compro eu* (<Lat>[ˈkõpɾu ew]</Lat> - I'll buy it).

**Friends use *tu*.** *Gosta de galão, Luísa?* sounds stiff between friends. They say *Gostas do galão?* (<Lat>[ˈɡɔʃtɐʒ du ɡɐˈlɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - do you like the galão?) or skip the verb: *Então, que tal está o galão?* (<Lat>[ẽˈtɐ̃w̃ &#124; kɨ taɫ ɨʃˈta u ɡɐˈlɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - so, how's the galão?)

**Half past, always *e meia*.** In speech nobody says *oito e trinta* (eight thirty); it is *oito e meia*.

**Slang.** *Ficar a ver navios* (<Lat>[fiˈkaɾ ɐ veɾ nɐˈviuʃ]</Lat> - to miss out, literally "to stay watching ships"); *fazer questão* (<Lat>[fɐˈzeɾ kɨʃˈtɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - to insist); *não se fala mais nisso* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ sɨ ˈfalɐ majʒ ˈnisu]</Lat> - say no more).
