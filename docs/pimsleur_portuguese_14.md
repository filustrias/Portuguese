# Pimsleur — Lesson 14

## Dialogue

A man wants to eat at noon, not at a quarter to eleven, and offers to pay.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Quero comer ao meio-dia. Eu não quero comer a um quarto para as onze. | [ˈkɛɾu kuˈmeɾ aw ˈmɐju ˈdiɐ ‖ ew nɐ̃w̃ ˈkɛɾu kuˈmeɾ ɐ ũ ˈkwaɾtu ˈpɐɾɐ ɐz ˈõzɨ] | I want to eat at noon. I don't want to eat at a quarter to eleven. |
| Woman | Às onze e um quarto? | [az ˈõzɨ i ũ ˈkwaɾtu] | At a quarter past eleven? |
| Man | Não, a um quarto para as onze. | [nɐ̃w̃ &#124; ɐ ũ ˈkwaɾtu ˈpɐɾɐ ɐz ˈõzɨ] | No, at a quarter to eleven. |
| Woman | Ah, é agora? | [a &#124; ɛ ɐˈɡɔɾɐ] | Oh, is that now? |
| Man | Sim. Tem tempo agora? | [sĩ ‖ tɐ̃j̃ ˈtẽpu ɐˈɡɔɾɐ] | Yes. Do you have time now? |
| Woman | Sim, tenho tempo, mas não tenho dinheiro. | [sĩ &#124; ˈtɐɲu ˈtẽpu &#124; mɐʒ nɐ̃w̃ ˈtɐɲu diˈɲɐjɾu] | Yes, I have time, but I don't have any money. |
| Man | Vamos comer alguma coisa? Eu pago. Posso pagar? | [ˈvɐmuʃ kuˈmeɾ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ ‖ ew ˈpaɡu ‖ ˈpɔsu pɐˈɡaɾ] | Shall we eat something? I'll pay. May I pay? |
| Woman | Ah, sim. Está bem. Muito obrigada. É muito gentil. | [a &#124; sĩ ‖ ɨʃˈta bɐ̃j̃ ‖ ˈmũj̃tu ɔbɾiˈɡadɐ ‖ ɛ ˈmũj̃tu ʒẽˈtiɫ] | Oh, yes. All right. Thank you very much. That's very kind. |

## Concepts

**What time is it?** *Que horas são?* (<Lat>[kɨ ˈɔɾɐʃ sɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - What time is it?) is plural, and so is the answer: *São três horas.* (<Lat>[sɐ̃w̃ tɾez ˈɔɾɐʃ]</Lat> - It's three o'clock.), *São três e meia.* (<Lat>[sɐ̃w̃ tɾez i ˈmɐjɐ]</Lat> - It's half past three.), *São um quarto para as quatro.* (<Lat>[sɐ̃w̃ ũ ˈkwaɾtu ˈpɐɾɐ ɐʃ ˈkwatɾu]</Lat> - It's a quarter to four.), *São um quarto para o meio-dia.* (<Lat>[sɐ̃w̃ ũ ˈkwaɾtu ˈpɐɾɐ u ˈmɐju ˈdiɐ]</Lat> - It's a quarter to twelve.) In everyday speech you will also hear *É um quarto para as quatro*.

**Early and late.** *Cedo* (<Lat>[ˈsedu]</Lat> - early) and *tarde* (<Lat>[ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - late): *É cedo.* (<Lat>[ɛ ˈsedu]</Lat> - It's early.), *É muito cedo.* (<Lat>[ɛ ˈmũj̃tu ˈsedu]</Lat> - It's very early.), *É tarde.* (<Lat>[ɛ ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - It's late.), *É muito tarde.* (<Lat>[ɛ ˈmũj̃tu ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - It's very late.) *Mais tarde* (<Lat>[majʃ ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - later), *mais cedo* (<Lat>[majʃ ˈsedu]</Lat> - earlier). *Tarde* is also "afternoon", as in *boa tarde*.

**For that.** *Não tenho tempo para isso.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ˈtɐɲu ˈtẽpu ˈpɐɾɐ ˈisu]</Lat> - I don't have time for that.), *Não tenho tempo para isso agora.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ˈtɐɲu ˈtẽpu ˈpɐɾɐ ˈisu ɐˈɡɔɾɐ]</Lat> - I don't have time for that now.)

**Too, also.** *Também* (<Lat>[tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - also, too): *Eu também.* (<Lat>[ew tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - Me too.), *Você também.* (<Lat>[vɔˈse tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - You too.), *Também quero um.* (<Lat>[tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j̃ ˈkɛɾu ũ]</Lat> - I want one too.)

**Kind.** *É muito gentil.* (<Lat>[ɛ ˈmũj̃tu ʒẽˈtiɫ]</Lat> - That's very kind of you.)

**Numbers from twenty.** Tens and units are joined by *e* (<Lat>[i]</Lat> - and):

| Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| vinte | [ˈvĩtɨ] | twenty |
| vinte e um | [ˈvĩtɨ i ũ] | twenty-one |
| vinte e dois | [ˈvĩtɨ i dojʃ] | twenty-two |
| vinte e quatro | [ˈvĩtɨ i ˈkwatɾu] | twenty-four |
| vinte e cinco | [ˈvĩtɨ i ˈsĩku] | twenty-five |
| vinte e oito | [ˈvĩtɨ i ˈojtu] | twenty-eight |
| vinte e nove | [ˈvĩtɨ i ˈnɔvɨ] | twenty-nine |
| trinta | [ˈtɾĩtɐ] | thirty |
| trinta e dois | [ˈtɾĩtɐ i dojʃ] | thirty-two |
| trinta e cinco | [ˈtɾĩtɐ i ˈsĩku] | thirty-five |

In fast speech *vinte e um* sounds like [ˈvĩti ũ].

## Exercises

1. Ask what time it is.
2. Say that it's half past three.
3. Say that it's very late.
4. Say that you don't have time for that now.
5. Say *Me too.*

::: details Answers
1. Que horas são?
2. São três e meia.
3. É muito tarde.
4. Não tenho tempo para isso agora.
5. Eu também.
:::

| Woman | Ah, sendo assim alinho já! Muito obrigada, és impecável! | [a &#124; ˈsẽdu ɐˈsĩ ɐˈliɲu ʒa ‖ ˈmũj̃tu ɔbɾiˈɡadɐ &#124; ɛz ĩpɨˈkavɛɫ] | Oh, in that case I'm in! Thanks a lot, you're a star! |

**Offering to pay.** Asking permission, *posso pagar?*, sounds clumsy. The offer is made as a statement: *Não te preocupes com o dinheiro, convido eu!* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ tɨ pɾiɔˈkupɨʒ kõ u diˈɲɐjɾu &#124; kõˈvidu ew]</Lat> - don't worry about the money, it's my treat!), *Deixa isso por minha conta.* (<Lat>[ˈdɐjʃɐ ˈisu puɾ ˈmiɲɐ ˈkõtɐ]</Lat> - leave that to me.)

**Thanking.** *É muito gentil* sounds like a translation of "that's very kind". In Portugal people say *Olhe, muito obrigada, é muito simpático da sua parte!* (<Lat>[ˈɔʎɨ &#124; ˈmũj̃tu ɔbɾiˈɡadɐ &#124; ɛ ˈmũj̃tu sĩˈpatiku dɐ ˈsuɐ ˈpaɾtɨ]</Lat> - thank you so much, that's very kind of you!) or, informally, *Obrigada, és um querido!* (<Lat>[ɔbɾiˈɡadɐ &#124; ɛz ũ kɨˈɾidu]</Lat> - thanks, you're a sweetheart!)

**No time.** *Não tenho tempo para isso agora* can sound sharp. Soften it: *Agora estou mesmo sem tempo nenhum.* (<Lat>[ɐˈɡɔɾɐ ɨʃˈto ˈmeʒmu sɐ̃j̃ ˈtẽpu nɨˈɲũ]</Lat> - right now I really have no time at all.), *Agora estou a correr, falamos depois.* (<Lat>[ɐˈɡɔɾɐ ɨʃˈto ɐ kuˈʁeɾ &#124; fɐˈlɐmuʒ dɨˈpojʃ]</Lat> - I'm in a rush right now, let's talk later.)

**Asking a stranger the time.** *Que horas são?* is correct, but the polite question in the street is *Desculpe, tem horas?* (<Lat>[dɨʃˈkuɫpɨ &#124; tɐ̃j̃ ˈɔɾɐʃ]</Lat> - excuse me, do you have the time?) or *Sabe-me dizer as horas, por favor?* (<Lat>[ˈsabɨmɨ diˈzeɾ ɐz ˈɔɾɐʃ &#124; puɾ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - could you tell me the time, please?)

**Slang.** *Sem um tostão* (<Lat>[sɐ̃j̃ ũ tuʃˈtɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - without a penny; the *tostão* was an old coin); *em ponto* (<Lat>[ɐ̃j̃ ˈpõtu]</Lat> - on the dot); *Mas homem...* (<Lat>[mɐz ˈɔmɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - but, man...); *és impecável* (<Lat>[ɛz ĩpɨˈkavɛɫ]</Lat> - you're the best, literally "you're impeccable"); *não stresses* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ʃˈtɾɛsɨʃ]</Lat> - don't stress).
