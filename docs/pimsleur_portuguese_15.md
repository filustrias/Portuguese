# Pimsleur — Lesson 15

## Dialogue

A man wants to talk, but the woman has no time today.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Pode conversar agora? Quero conversar um pouco. | [ˈpɔdɨ kõvɨɾˈsaɾ ɐˈɡɔɾɐ ‖ ˈkɛɾu kõvɨɾˈsaɾ ũ ˈpoku] | Can you talk now? I'd like to talk a little. |
| Woman | Que horas são? | [kɨ ˈɔɾɐʃ sɐ̃w̃] | What time is it? |
| Man | São um quarto para o meio-dia. | [sɐ̃w̃ ũ ˈkwaɾtu ˈpɐɾɐ u ˈmɐju ˈdiɐ] | It's a quarter to twelve. |
| Woman | Não, desculpe, não posso. É muito tarde. Não há tempo agora. | [nɐ̃w̃ &#124; dɨʃˈkuɫpɨ &#124; nɐ̃w̃ ˈpɔsu ‖ ɛ ˈmũj̃tu ˈtaɾdɨ ‖ nɐ̃w̃ a ˈtẽpu ɐˈɡɔɾɐ] | No, sorry, I can't. It's very late. There's no time now. |
| Man | E mais tarde? Tem tempo mais tarde? | [i majʃ ˈtaɾdɨ ‖ tɐ̃j̃ ˈtẽpu majʃ ˈtaɾdɨ] | And later? Do you have time later? |
| Woman | Não sei. | [nɐ̃w̃ sɐj] | I don't know. |
| Man | E amanhã? Pode conversar amanhã? | [i ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃ ‖ ˈpɔdɨ kõvɨɾˈsaɾ ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃] | And tomorrow? Can you talk tomorrow? |
| Woman | Sim, está bem. Vamos conversar amanhã. | [sĩ &#124; ɨʃˈta bɐ̃j̃ ‖ ˈvɐmuʃ kõvɨɾˈsaɾ ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃] | Yes, all right. Let's talk tomorrow. |

## Concepts

**With you, formal.** *Conversar* (<Lat>[kõvɨɾˈsaɾ]</Lat> - to talk, to chat). *Com* + *o senhor* / *a senhora* becomes *consigo* (<Lat>[kõˈsiɡu]</Lat> - with you, formal): *Quero conversar consigo.* (<Lat>[ˈkɛɾu kõvɨɾˈsaɾ kõˈsiɡu]</Lat> - I want to talk with you.), *Quero comer consigo.* (<Lat>[ˈkɛɾu kuˈmeɾ kõˈsiɡu]</Lat> - I want to eat with you.)

**Together and alone.** *Juntos* (<Lat>[ˈʒũtuʃ]</Lat> - together): *Vamos juntos.* (<Lat>[ˈvɐmuʒ ˈʒũtuʃ]</Lat> - Let's go together.), *Nós vamos comprar alguma coisa juntos.* (<Lat>[nɔʒ ˈvɐmuʃ kõˈpɾaɾ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ ˈʒũtuʃ]</Lat> - We're going to buy something together.) A woman alone says *sozinha* (<Lat>[sɔˈziɲɐ]</Lat> - alone): *Vou sozinha.* (<Lat>[vo sɔˈziɲɐ]</Lat> - I'm going alone.) A man says *sozinho*.

**He and she.** *Ele* (<Lat>[ˈelɨ]</Lat> - he) and *ela* (<Lat>[ˈɛlɐ]</Lat> - she): *Ele tem euros.* (<Lat>[ˈelɨ tɐ̃j̃ ˈewɾuʃ]</Lat> - He has euros.), *Ele sabe.* (<Lat>[ˈelɨ ˈsabɨ]</Lat> - He knows.), *O Carlos não está aqui.* (<Lat>[u ˈkaɾluʒ nɐ̃w̃ ɨʃˈta ɐˈki]</Lat> - Carlos isn't here.) Names take the article, also after prepositions: *com o Carlos* (<Lat>[kõ u ˈkaɾluʃ]</Lat> - with Carlos), *Não gosto do Carlos.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ˈɡɔʃtu du ˈkaɾluʃ]</Lat> - I don't like Carlos.)

**Who.** *Quem* (<Lat>[kɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - who): *Quem sabe?* (<Lat>[kɐ̃j̃ ˈsabɨ]</Lat> - Who knows?), *Quem é que vai pagar?* (<Lat>[kɐ̃j̃ ɛ kɨ vaj pɐˈɡaɾ]</Lat> - Who's going to pay?)

**To do.** *Fazer* (<Lat>[fɐˈzeɾ]</Lat> - to do, to make): *O que é que nós vamos fazer?* (<Lat>[u kɨ ɛ kɨ nɔʒ ˈvɐmuʃ fɐˈzeɾ]</Lat> - What are we going to do?), *Não vou fazer nada.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ vo fɐˈzeɾ ˈnadɐ]</Lat> - I'm not going to do anything.)

**Too much.** *Demais* (<Lat>[dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat> - too much) goes after the verb or noun: *É demais.* (<Lat>[ɛ dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat> - It's too much.), *Como demais.* (<Lat>[ˈkomu dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat> - I eat too much.), *Bebo demais.* (<Lat>[ˈbebu dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat> - I drink too much.), *tempo demais* (<Lat>[ˈtẽpu dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat> - too much time).

**Numbers.** *quarenta* (<Lat>[kwɐˈɾẽtɐ]</Lat> - forty), *quarenta e cinco* (<Lat>[kwɐˈɾẽtɐ i ˈsĩku]</Lat> - forty-five), *cinquenta* (<Lat>[sĩˈkwẽtɐ]</Lat> - fifty), *cinquenta e três* (<Lat>[sĩˈkwẽtɐ i tɾeʃ]</Lat> - fifty-three).

## Exercises

1. Ask someone (formal) if they can talk now.
2. Say that you want to talk with them (formal).
3. Ask who is going to pay.
4. Say that you are not going to do anything.
5. Say that it is too much.

::: details Answers
1. Pode conversar agora?
2. Quero conversar consigo.
3. Quem é que vai pagar?
4. Não vou fazer nada.
5. É demais.
:::

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Olha, desculpa, tens dois minutinhos? Queria dar-te uma palavrinha rápida. | [ˈɔʎɐ &#124; dɨʃˈkuɫpɐ &#124; tɐ̃j̃ʒ dojʒ minuˈtiɲuʃ ‖ kɨˈɾiɐ ˈdaɾtɨ ˈumɐ pɐlɐˈvɾiɲɐ ˈʁapidɐ] | Hey, sorry, have you got a couple of minutes? I wanted a quick word. |
| Woman | Que horas tens aí? | [kɨ ˈɔɾɐʃ tɐ̃j̃z ɐˈi] | What time have you got? |
| Man | Falta aí um quarto para o meio-dia. | [ˈfaɫtɐ ɐˈi ũ ˈkwaɾtu ˈpɐɾɐ u ˈmɐju ˈdiɐ] | It's about a quarter to twelve. |
| Woman | Eh pá, agora não posso de todo, estou mesmo sem tempo nenhum! | [ɛ pa &#124; ɐˈɡɔɾɐ nɐ̃w̃ ˈpɔsu dɨ ˈtodu &#124; ɨʃˈto ˈmeʒmu sɐ̃j̃ ˈtẽpu nɨˈɲũ] | Oh man, I really can't right now, I've got no time at all! |
| Man | E mais para o fim do dia, consegues? | [i majʃ ˈpɐɾɐ u fĩ du ˈdiɐ &#124; kõˈsɛɡɨʃ] | And towards the end of the day, can you? |
| Woman | Hoje vai ser complicado... | [ˈoʒɨ vaj seɾ kõpliˈkadu] | Today's going to be tricky... |
| Man | E amanhã, dá-te jeito? | [i ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃ &#124; ˈdatɨ ˈʒɐjtu] | And tomorrow, does that suit you? |
| Woman | Sim, amanhã por mim é tranquilo. Falamos com tempo! | [sĩ &#124; ɐmɐˈɲɐ̃ puɾ mĩ ɛ tɾɐ̃ˈkwilu ‖ fɐˈlɐmuʃ kõ ˈtẽpu] | Yes, tomorrow's fine by me. We'll talk properly then! |

**Falar, not conversar.** To ask for a moment of someone's time, Portugal uses *falar* (<Lat>[fɐˈlaɾ]</Lat> - to speak): *Importa-se que falemos dois minutos?* (<Lat>[ĩˈpɔɾtɐsɨ kɨ fɐˈlemuʒ dojʒ miˈnutuʃ]</Lat> - would you mind if we talked for two minutes?, formal), *Tens um segundo para falar?* (<Lat>[tɐ̃j̃z ũ sɨˈɡũdu ˈpɐɾɐ fɐˈlaɾ]</Lat> - got a second to talk?, informal).

**Consigo is formal only.** Among colleagues and friends, *consigo* sounds odd. Use *contigo* (<Lat>[kõˈtiɡu]</Lat> - with you, informal): *Queria falar contigo.* (<Lat>[kɨˈɾiɐ fɐˈlaɾ kõˈtiɡu]</Lat> - I wanted to talk to you.), *Posso falar contigo um bocadinho?* (<Lat>[ˈpɔsu fɐˈlaɾ kõˈtiɡu ũ bukɐˈdiɲu]</Lat> - can I talk to you for a bit?)

**No time.** *Não há tempo* sounds bureaucratic. People say *Olhe, agora não consigo mesmo, estou sem tempo nenhum.* (<Lat>[ˈɔʎɨ &#124; ɐˈɡɔɾɐ nɐ̃w̃ kõˈsiɡu ˈmeʒmu &#124; ɨʃˈto sɐ̃j̃ ˈtẽpu nɨˈɲũ]</Lat> - I really can't right now, I've got no time at all.)

***É demais!*, careful.** In the lesson *é demais* means "it's too much". In casual speech, *É demais!* (<Lat>[ɛ dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat>) or *Tu és demais!* (<Lat>[tu ɛʒ dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat> - you're amazing!) is very often praise. To complain about a price or an amount, say *É um exagero!* (<Lat>[ɛ ũ izɐˈʒeɾu]</Lat> - that's way too much!)

**Slang.** *Eh pá* (<Lat>[ɛ pa]</Lat> - oh man, gosh), a very common Portuguese interjection; *dar uma palavrinha* (<Lat>[daɾ ˈumɐ pɐlɐˈvɾiɲɐ]</Lat> - to have a quick word); *é tranquilo* (<Lat>[ɛ tɾɐ̃ˈkwilu]</Lat> - it's no problem, it's easy).
