# Pimsleur — Lesson 16

## Dialogue

A woman invites João to the beer hall.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Woman | Bom dia, João. Como está? | [bõ ˈdiɐ &#124; ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ ‖ ˈkomu ɨʃˈta] | Good morning, João. How are you? |
| João | Muito bem, obrigado. E você? | [ˈmũj̃tu bɐ̃j̃ &#124; ɔbɾiˈɡadu ‖ i vɔˈse] | Very well, thank you. And you? |
| Woman | Muito bem, também. Vou à cervejaria. | [ˈmũj̃tu bɐ̃j̃ &#124; tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j̃ ‖ vo a sɨɾvɨʒɐˈɾiɐ] | Very well too. I'm going to the beer hall. |
| João | Com quem é que vai? | [kõ kɐ̃j̃ ɛ kɨ vaj] | Who are you going with? |
| Woman | Consigo. Vamos beber cerveja? | [kõˈsiɡu ‖ ˈvɐmuʒ bɨˈbeɾ sɨɾˈvɐʒɐ] | With you. Shall we drink some beer? |
| João | Sim, obrigado. E quero comer caracóis e tremoços. | [sĩ &#124; ɔbɾiˈɡadu ‖ i ˈkɛɾu kuˈmeɾ kɐɾɐˈkɔjz i tɾɨˈmɔsuʃ] | Yes, thanks. And I want to eat snails and lupini beans. |

## Concepts

**A cervejaria.** *Uma cervejaria* (<Lat>[ˈumɐ sɨɾvɨʒɐˈɾiɐ]</Lat> - a beer hall) serves draught beer, seafood, steak sandwiches and counter snacks. *Caracóis* (<Lat>[kɐɾɐˈkɔjʃ]</Lat> - small snails cooked in broth) and *tremoços* (<Lat>[tɾɨˈmɔsuʃ]</Lat> - salted lupini beans) are the classic spring and summer pairing with a beer.

**With me, with us.** *Com* merges with the pronoun:

| Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| comigo | [kuˈmiɡu] | with me |
| contigo | [kõˈtiɡu] | with you (informal) |
| consigo | [kõˈsiɡu] | with you (formal) |
| connosco | [kõˈnoʃku] | with us |

*Connosco* is spelt with double *n* in Portugal (Brazil writes *conosco*).

**My wife, my husband.** In Portugal the possessive takes the article: *a minha mulher* (<Lat>[ɐ ˈmiɲɐ muˈʎɛɾ]</Lat> - my wife), *a sua mulher* (<Lat>[ɐ ˈsuɐ muˈʎɛɾ]</Lat> - your wife), *o meu marido* (<Lat>[u ˈmew mɐˈɾidu]</Lat> - my husband), *o seu marido* (<Lat>[u ˈsew mɐˈɾidu]</Lat> - your husband), *o meu amigo* (<Lat>[u ˈmew ɐˈmiɡu]</Lat> - my friend), *a minha amiga* (<Lat>[ɐ ˈmiɲɐ ɐˈmiɡɐ]</Lat> - my friend, a woman). Plurals: *os meus amigos* (<Lat>[uʒ ˈmewz ɐˈmiɡuʃ]</Lat> - my friends), *as minhas amigas* (<Lat>[ɐʒ ˈmiɲɐz ɐˈmiɡɐʃ]</Lat>).

**Women.** *A mulher* → *as mulheres* (<Lat>[ɐʒ muˈʎɛɾɨʃ]</Lat> - the women). *Mulher* means both "woman" and "wife". The pronoun for them is *elas* (<Lat>[ˈɛlɐʃ]</Lat> - they, feminine).

**We.** *Nós temos* (<Lat>[nɔʃ ˈtemuʃ]</Lat> - we have), *nós queremos* (<Lat>[nɔʃ kɨˈɾemuʃ]</Lat> - we want).

**Numbers.** *sessenta* (<Lat>[sɨˈsẽtɐ]</Lat> - sixty), *sessenta e cinco* (<Lat>[sɨˈsẽtɐ i ˈsĩku]</Lat> - sixty-five), *setenta* (<Lat>[sɨˈtẽtɐ]</Lat> - seventy), *setenta e três* (<Lat>[sɨˈtẽtɐ i tɾeʃ]</Lat> - seventy-three), *setenta e nove* (<Lat>[sɨˈtẽtɐ i ˈnɔvɨ]</Lat> - seventy-nine).

## Exercises

1. Say that you are going to the beer hall.
2. Ask someone (formal) who they are going with.
3. Invite someone (formal) to drink a beer with you.
4. Say *my wife* and *my husband*.
5. Ask if they want to come with us.

::: details Answers
1. Vou à cervejaria.
2. Com quem é que vai?
3. Vamos beber uma cerveja?
4. A minha mulher, o meu marido.
5. Quer vir connosco?
:::

|:--|:--|:--|:--|

I'm in, and order a saucer of lupini beans and a little plate of snails too, to whet the appetite! |

**Never just *cerveja*.** Proposing to "drink beer" in general gives you away as a foreigner. A draught beer in a glass has regional names: *uma imperial* (<Lat>[ˈumɐ ĩpɨɾiˈaɫ]</Lat> - a small draught beer) in Lisbon and the south, *um fino* (<Lat>[ũ ˈfinu]</Lat>) in Porto and the north, and *uma jola* (<Lat>[ˈumɐ ˈʒɔlɐ]</Lat> - a beer, slang) or *uma cervejinha* (<Lat>[ˈumɐ sɨɾvɐˈʒiɲɐ]</Lat> - a nice little beer) everywhere: *Bora ali à cervejaria beber uma jola e comer um prato de caracóis?* (<Lat>[ˈbɔɾɐ ɐˈli a sɨɾvɨʒɐˈɾiɐ bɨˈbeɾ ˈumɐ ˈʒɔlɐ i kuˈmeɾ ũ ˈpɾatu dɨ kɐɾɐˈkɔjʃ]</Lat> - shall we go to the beer hall for a beer and a plate of snails?)

**Mulher, not esposa.** *A minha mulher* and *o meu marido* are the natural words at every level of society. *Esposa* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈpozɐ]</Lat> - spouse) sounds ceremonious or bureaucratic.

**Paying a round.** Among friends in a beer hall: *Pagas tu esta e pago eu a seguinte.* (<Lat>[ˈpaɡɐʃ tu ˈɛʃtɐ i ˈpaɡu ew ɐ sɨˈɡĩtɨ]</Lat> - you get this one and I'll get the next.), *Rachamos a conta a meias.* (<Lat>[ʁɐˈʃɐmuz ɐ ˈkõtɐ ɐ ˈmɐjɐʃ]</Lat> - let's split the bill half and half.)

**Ter com.** *Ir ter com alguém* (<Lat>[iɾ teɾ kõ aɫˈɡɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - to go and meet someone) is the everyday way to say you are meeting someone: *Vais ter com quem?* (<Lat>[vajʃ teɾ kõ kɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - who are you meeting?)

**Slang.** *Tudo em cima?* (<Lat>[ˈtudu ɐ̃j̃ ˈsimɐ]</Lat> - all good?, literally "everything on top?"); *estar virado para* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈtaɾ viˈɾadu ˈpɐɾɐ]</Lat> - to be up for, to feel like); *manda vir* (<Lat>[ˈmɐ̃dɐ viɾ]</Lat> - order it, literally "send for it"); *um pires* (<Lat>[ũ ˈpiɾɨʃ]</Lat> - a saucer, the standard serving of *tremoços*).
