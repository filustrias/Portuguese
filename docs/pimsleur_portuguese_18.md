# Pimsleur — Lesson 18

## Dialogue

An American man looks for a present for his wife in a shop.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Boa tarde. Quero comprar alguma coisa para a minha mulher. Somos americanos. Quero comprar alguma coisa de Portugal. | [ˈboɐ ˈtaɾdɨ ‖ ˈkɛɾu kõˈpɾaɾ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ ˈpɐɾɐ ɐ ˈmiɲɐ muˈʎɛɾ ‖ ˈsomuz ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnuʃ ‖ ˈkɛɾu kõˈpɾaɾ aɫˈɡumɐ ˈkojzɐ dɨ puɾtuˈɡaɫ] | Good afternoon. I want to buy something for my wife. We're American. I want to buy something from Portugal. |
| Shop assistant | Do que é que o senhor gosta? | [du kɨ ɛ kɨ u sɨˈɲoɾ ˈɡɔʃtɐ] | What do you like? |
| Man | Não sei. Uma coisa boa. | [nɐ̃w̃ sɐj ‖ ˈumɐ ˈkojzɐ ˈboɐ] | I don't know. Something good. |

| Man | Não sei. Talvez. Ah, aqui está a minha mulher. Vou falar com ela. | [nɐ̃w̃ sɐj ‖ taɫˈveʃ ‖ a &#124; ɐˈki ɨʃˈta ɐ ˈmiɲɐ muˈʎɛɾ ‖ vo fɐˈlaɾ kõ ˈɛlɐ] | I don't know. Maybe. Ah, here's my wife. I'll talk to her. |

## Concepts

**Spending.** *Gastar* (<Lat>[ɡɐʃˈtaɾ]</Lat> - to spend): *Quanto é que quer gastar?* (<Lat>[ˈkwɐ̃tu ɛ kɨ kɛɾ ɡɐʃˈtaɾ]</Lat> - How much do you want to spend?) *Aqui está* (<Lat>[ɐˈki ɨʃˈta]</Lat> - here it is) when you hand something over.

**Enough.** *Suficiente* (<Lat>[sufisiˈẽtɨ]</Lat> - enough): *Tenho dinheiro suficiente.* (<Lat>[ˈtɐɲu diˈɲɐjɾu sufisiˈẽtɨ]</Lat> - I have enough money.), *Não é suficiente.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ɛ sufisiˈẽtɨ]</Lat> - It's not enough.), *Para nós é suficiente.* (<Lat>[ˈpɐɾɐ nɔz ɛ sufisiˈẽtɨ]</Lat> - For us it's enough.)

**Ser or estar.** Both mean "to be":

- *ser* for what something is: identity, nationality, lasting traits. *Sou americano.* (<Lat>[so ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnu]</Lat> - I'm American.), *Somos amigos.* (<Lat>[ˈsomuz ɐˈmiɡuʃ]</Lat> - We're friends.), *A minha mulher é americana.* (<Lat>[ɐ ˈmiɲɐ muˈʎɛɾ ɛ ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnɐ]</Lat> - My wife is American.);
- *estar* for a passing state or a location. *Estou sozinho.* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈto sɔˈziɲu]</Lat> - I'm alone.), *A minha mulher não está aqui.* (<Lat>[ɐ ˈmiɲɐ muˈʎɛɾ nɐ̃w̃ ɨʃˈta ɐˈki]</Lat> - My wife isn't here.), *Estamos em Portugal.* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈtɐmuz ɐ̃j̃ puɾtuˈɡaɫ]</Lat> - We're in Portugal.), *Estou em Lisboa.* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈto ɐ̃j̃ liʒˈboɐ]</Lat> - I'm in Lisbon.), *no Algarve* (<Lat>[nu aɫˈɡaɾvɨ]</Lat> - in the Algarve).

**Ready.** *Estar pronto* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈtaɾ ˈpɾõtu]</Lat> - to be ready) agrees with the person: *Está pronto?* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈta ˈpɾõtu]</Lat> - Are you ready?, to a man), *Estou pronta.* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈto ˈpɾõtɐ]</Lat> - I'm ready, said by a woman), *Estamos prontos.* (<Lat>[ɨʃˈtɐmuʃ ˈpɾõtuʃ]</Lat> - We're ready.)

**Going away.** *Ir-se embora* (<Lat>[ˈiɾsɨ ẽˈbɔɾɐ]</Lat> - to leave, to go away) takes a pronoun after the verb: *Eu vou-me embora.* (<Lat>[ew ˈvomɨ ẽˈbɔɾɐ]</Lat> - I'm leaving.), *Nós vamos-nos embora.* (<Lat>[nɔʒ ˈvɐmuʒnuz ẽˈbɔɾɐ]</Lat> - We're leaving.)

## Exercises

1. Say that you want to buy something for your wife.
2. Ask a man (formal) how much he wants to spend.
3. Say that your wife is American, but that she isn't here.
4. Say, as a woman, that you're ready.
5. Say that you're leaving.

::: details Answers
1. Quero comprar alguma coisa para a minha mulher.
2. Quanto é que o senhor quer gastar?
3. A minha mulher é americana, mas não está aqui.
4. Estou pronta.
5. Vou-me embora.
:::

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Boa tarde! Agrada-lhe? | [kõ sɨɾˈtezɐ ‖ ˈɔʎɨ &#124; ˈvɐʒɐ la ˈɛʃtɐ ˈpɛsɐ ɐˈki ‖ ɛ ɐɾtɨzɐˈnatu ˈtipiku i ˈfikɐ puɾ nuˈvẽtɐ ˈewɾuʃ ‖ ɐˈɡɾadɐʎɨ] | Of course! Have a look at this piece here. It's typical handicraft and comes to ninety euros. Do you like it? |
| Man | É bonita, sim senhor... Olhe, ela vem mesmo aí, vou só perguntar-lhe o que é que ela acha e já falamos! | [ɛ buˈnitɐ &#124; sĩ sɨˈɲoɾ ‖ ˈɔʎɨ &#124; ˈɛlɐ vɐ̃j̃ ˈmeʒmu ɐˈi &#124; vo sɔ pɨɾɡũˈtaɾʎɨ u kɨ ɛ kɨ ˈɛlɐ ˈaʃɐ i ʒa fɐˈlɐmuʃ] | It's pretty, it really is... Look, here she comes, I'll just ask her what she thinks and get back to you! |

**Looking for a souvenir.** In souvenir and craft shops in Lisbon, Sintra or Porto, customers say *Andava à procura de uma lembrança típica para levar à minha mulher.* (<Lat>[ɐ̃ˈdavɐ a pɾɔˈkuɾɐ dɨ ˈumɐ lẽˈbɾɐ̃sɐ ˈtipikɐ ˈpɐɾɐ lɨˈvaɾ a ˈmiɲɐ muˈʎɛɾ]</Lat> - I was looking for a typical souvenir to take to my wife.) or *Queria levar uma prenda tradicional.* (<Lat>[kɨˈɾiɐ lɨˈvaɾ ˈumɐ ˈpɾẽdɐ tɾɐdisiuˈnaɫ]</Lat> - I'd like to take a traditional present.) The imperfect *andava*, like *era* and *queria*, softens the request.

**Talking about price.** A good shop assistant does not ask *quanto quer gastar?* outright: *Até que valor é que estava a pensar, mais ou menos?* (<Lat>[ɐˈtɛ kɨ vɐˈloɾ ɛ kɨ ɨʃˈtavɐ ɐ pẽˈsaɾ &#124; majz o ˈmenuʃ]</Lat> - up to how much were you thinking, roughly?), *Tem algum orçamento em mente?* (<Lat>[tɐ̃j̃ aɫˈɡũ ɔɾsɐˈmẽtu ɐ̃j̃ ˈmẽtɨ]</Lat> - do you have a budget in mind?)

**Showing an item.** Taking something off the shelf, the shopkeeper says *Ora veja lá esta peça.* (<Lat>[ˈɔɾɐ ˈvɐʒɐ la ˈɛʃtɐ ˈpɛsɐ]</Lat> - now have a look at this piece.) or *Ora aqui tem.* (<Lat>[ˈɔɾɐ ɐˈki tɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - here you are.)

**Buying time.** Before deciding: *Deixe-me só ver o que é que ela acha.* (<Lat>[ˈdɐjʃɨmɨ sɔ veɾ u kɨ ɛ kɨ ˈɛlɐ ˈaʃɐ]</Lat> - let me just see what she thinks.), *Já lhe digo qualquer coisa.* (<Lat>[ʒa ʎɨ ˈdiɡu kwaɫˈkɛɾ ˈkojzɐ]</Lat> - I'll let you know in a moment.)

**Slang.** *Uma lembrancinha* (<Lat>[ˈumɐ lẽbɾɐ̃ˈsiɲɐ]</Lat> - a little souvenir); *sim senhor* (<Lat>[sĩ sɨˈɲoɾ]</Lat> - it really is, said to agree warmly with anyone, man or woman); *levar à falência* (<Lat>[lɨˈvaɾ a fɐˈlẽsiɐ]</Lat> - to bankrupt).
