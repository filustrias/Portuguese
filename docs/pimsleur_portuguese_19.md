# Pimsleur — Lesson 19

## Dialogue

A man invites Beatriz to a seafood restaurant, but she has only half an hour.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Olá, Beatriz. Vai à marisqueira comigo? | [ɔˈla &#124; biɐˈtɾiʃ ‖ vaj a mɐɾiʃˈkɐjɾɐ kuˈmiɡu] | Hi, Beatriz. Will you come to the seafood restaurant with me? |
| Beatriz | Sim, vou e quero comer. | [sĩ &#124; vo i ˈkɛɾu kuˈmeɾ] | Yes, I'll come, and I want to eat. |
| Man | Que bom. E também quero beber um vinho verde. | [kɨ bõ ‖ i tɐ̃ˈbɐ̃j̃ ˈkɛɾu bɨˈbeɾ ũ ˈviɲu ˈveɾdɨ] | Great. And I also want to drink a vinho verde. |
| Beatriz | Mas só tenho trinta minutos. É suficiente? | [mɐʃ sɔ ˈtɐɲu ˈtɾĩtɐ miˈnutuʃ ‖ ɛ sufisiˈẽtɨ] | But I only have thirty minutes. Is that enough? |
| Man | Sim, sim, é suficiente. | [sĩ &#124; sĩ &#124; ɛ sufisiˈẽtɨ] | Yes, yes, it's enough. |

## Concepts

**A marisqueira.** *Uma marisqueira* (<Lat>[ˈumɐ mɐɾiʃˈkɐjɾɐ]</Lat> - a seafood restaurant) serves shellfish, such as lobster, crab, goose barnacles, clams and prawns, and fresh fish from the Portuguese coast. *Que bom!* (<Lat>[kɨ bõ]</Lat> - great!, how nice!)

**In an hour, in a week.** *Daqui a* (<Lat>[dɐˈki ɐ]</Lat> - in, from now) counts time forward from now:

| Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| daqui a uma hora | [dɐˈki ɐ ˈumɐ ˈɔɾɐ] | in an hour |
| daqui a uma semana | [dɐˈki ɐ ˈumɐ sɨˈmɐnɐ] | in a week |
| daqui a duas semanas | [dɐˈki ɐ ˈduɐʃ sɨˈmɐnɐʃ] | in two weeks |
| daqui a um mês | [dɐˈki ɐ ũ meʃ] | in a month |
| daqui a dois meses | [dɐˈki ɐ dojʒ ˈmezɨʃ] | in two months |
| daqui a quatro meses | [dɐˈki ɐ ˈkwatɾu ˈmezɨʃ] | in four months |

**In the afternoon.** *Da tarde* (<Lat>[dɐ ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - in the afternoon, p.m.): *às quatro da tarde* (<Lat>[aʃ ˈkwatɾu dɐ ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - at four in the afternoon), *às três da tarde* (<Lat>[aʃ tɾeʒ dɐ ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - at three in the afternoon), *às cinco da tarde* (<Lat>[aʃ ˈsĩku dɐ ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - at five in the afternoon).

**All of us.** *Todos* / *todas* (<Lat>[ˈtoduʃ] / [ˈtodɐʃ]</Lat> - all, everyone): *Falamos todos em português.* (<Lat>[fɐˈlɐmuʃ ˈtoduz ɐ̃j̃ puɾtuˈɡeʃ]</Lat> - We all speak in Portuguese.), *Vamos comer todos juntos.* (<Lat>[ˈvɐmuʃ kuˈmeɾ ˈtoduʒ ˈʒũtuʃ]</Lat> - Let's all eat together.), *Somos todos americanos.* (<Lat>[ˈsomuʃ ˈtoduz ɐmɨɾiˈkɐnuʃ]</Lat> - We're all American.)

**Not leaving.** As with *não o percebo*, *não* pulls the pronoun in front of the verb:

| Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|
| vou-me embora | [ˈvomɨ ẽˈbɔɾɐ] | I'm leaving |
| não me vou embora | [nɐ̃w̃ mɨ vo ẽˈbɔɾɐ] | I'm not leaving |
| vamos-nos embora | [ˈvɐmuʒnuz ẽˈbɔɾɐ] | we're leaving |
| não nos vamos embora | [nɐ̃w̃ nuʒ ˈvɐmuz ẽˈbɔɾɐ] | we're not leaving |
| não se vão embora | [nɐ̃w̃ sɨ vɐ̃w̃ ẽˈbɔɾɐ] | you're not leaving (plural); they're not leaving |

**Portuguese, plural.** *Amigas portuguesas* (<Lat>[ɐˈmiɡɐʃ puɾtuˈɡezɐʃ]</Lat> - Portuguese friends, women), *amigos portugueses* (<Lat>[ɐˈmiɡuʃ puɾtuˈɡezɨʃ]</Lat> - Portuguese friends).

**Numbers.** *oitocentos* (<Lat>[ojtuˈsẽtuʃ]</Lat> - eight hundred), *oitocentos e dezanove* (<Lat>[ojtuˈsẽtuz i dɨzɐˈnɔvɨ]</Lat> - eight hundred and nineteen), *oitocentos e oitenta e três* (<Lat>[ojtuˈsẽtuz i ojˈtẽtɐ i tɾeʃ]</Lat> - eight hundred and eighty-three).

## Exercises

1. Ask Beatriz (formal) if she will go to the seafood restaurant with you.
2. Say that you only have thirty minutes.
3. Ask if that's enough.
4. Say *in two weeks*.
5. Say that you're not leaving.

2. Só tenho trinta minutos.
3. É suficiente?
4. Daqui a duas semanas.

## Informal version

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Man | Olá, Beatriz! Ouve lá, apetece-te ir dar uma curva até à marisqueira? | [ɔˈla &#124; biɐˈtɾiʃ ‖ ˈovɨ la &#124; ɐpɨˈtɛsɨtɨ iɾ daɾ ˈumɐ ˈkuɾvɐ ɐˈtɛ a mɐɾiʃˈkɐjɾɐ] | Hi, Beatriz! Listen, fancy popping over to the seafood place? |
| Beatriz | Olha, por acaso vinha mesmo cheia de fome, alinho já! | [ˈɔʎɐ &#124; puɾ ɐˈkazu ˈviɲɐ ˈmeʒmu ˈʃɐjɐ dɨ ˈfɔmɨ &#124; ɐˈliɲu ʒa] | As it happens I'm starving, count me in! |
| Man | Maravilha! Pedimos um verde bem fresquinho a acompanhar. | [mɐɾɐˈviʎɐ ‖ pɨˈdimuz ũ ˈveɾdɨ bɐ̃j̃ fɾɨʃˈkiɲu ɐ ɐkõpɐˈɲaɾ] | Brilliant! We'll order a nice cold verde to go with it. |
| Beatriz | Mas olha que só tenho para aí uma meia horita... achas que chega? | [mɐz ˈɔʎɐ kɨ sɔ ˈtɐɲu ˈpɐɾɐ ɐˈi ˈumɐ ˈmɐjɐ ɔˈɾitɐ ‖ ˈaʃɐʃ kɨ ˈʃeɡɐ] | But mind you, I've only got about half an hour... do you think that's enough? |
| Man | Dá perfeitamente, comemos qualquer coisa ligeira e ficamos despachados num instante! | [da pɨɾfɐjtɐˈmẽtɨ &#124; kuˈmemuʃ kwaɫˈkɛɾ ˈkojzɐ liˈʒɐjɾɐ i fiˈkɐmuʒ dɨʃpɐˈʃaduʒ nũ iʃˈtɐ̃tɨ] | That's plenty, we'll have something light and be done in no time! |

**Inviting someone for seafood.** Going to a *marisqueira* is a relaxed social outing, and the invitation appeals to the appetite: *Olha, apetece-te ir ali a uma marisqueira almoçar?* (<Lat>[ˈɔʎɐ &#124; ɐpɨˈtɛsɨtɨ iɾ ɐˈli ɐ ˈumɐ mɐɾiʃˈkɐjɾɐ aɫmuˈsaɾ]</Lat> - fancy lunch at a seafood place?), *Bora ali petiscar qualquer coisa à marisqueira?* (<Lat>[ˈbɔɾɐ ɐˈli pɨtiʃˈkaɾ kwaɫˈkɛɾ ˈkojzɐ a mɐɾiʃˈkɐjɾɐ]</Lat> - shall we go and nibble something at the seafood place?)

**Enough? Chega.** *Suficiente* is correct but bookish. In speech people use *chegar* (<Lat>[ʃɨˈɡaɾ]</Lat> - to be enough, literally "to arrive") or *dar tempo* (<Lat>[daɾ ˈtẽpu]</Lat> - to be enough time): *Achas que chega?* (<Lat>[ˈaʃɐʃ kɨ ˈʃeɡɐ]</Lat> - do you think that's enough?), *Dá tempo ou temos de comer a correr?* (<Lat>[da ˈtẽpu o ˈtemuʒ dɨ kuˈmeɾ ɐ kuˈʁeɾ]</Lat> - is there time, or do we have to eat in a rush?)

**Reassuring.** The usual answers: *Chega e sobra!* (<Lat>[ˈʃeɡɐ i ˈsɔbɾɐ]</Lat> - that's more than enough!), *Dá perfeitamente!* (<Lat>[da pɨɾfɐjtɐˈmẽtɨ]</Lat> - that's plenty!), *Dá na boa!* (<Lat>[da nɐ ˈboɐ]</Lat> - no problem!)

**Half an hour.** For a break, people rarely say *trinta minutos*; they say *meia hora* (<Lat>[ˈmɐjɐ ˈɔɾɐ]</Lat> - half an hour) or, fondly, *uma meia horita* (<Lat>[ˈumɐ ˈmɐjɐ ɔˈɾitɐ]</Lat> - a quick half hour).

**Slang.** *Dar uma curva* (<Lat>[daɾ ˈumɐ ˈkuɾvɐ]</Lat> - to pop over somewhere, literally "to take a bend"); *maravilha!* (<Lat>[mɐɾɐˈviʎɐ]</Lat> - brilliant!); *ficar despachado* (<Lat>[fiˈkaɾ dɨʃpɐˈʃadu]</Lat> - to be done, to get it over with); *um verde* (<Lat>[ũ ˈveɾdɨ]</Lat> - a vinho verde).
