# Pimsleur — Lesson 20

## Dialogue

João meets Mariana, but she has to leave straight away.

| Who | Portuguese | IPA | English |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| João | Boa tarde, eu sou João Smith de Nova Iorque. | [ˈboɐ ˈtaɾdɨ &#124; ew so ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ smiθ dɨ ˈnɔvɐ ˈjɔɾkɨ] | Good afternoon, I'm João Smith from New York. |
| Mariana | Muito prazer, João. Eu sou Mariana Machado de Lisboa. | [ˈmũj̃tu pɾɐˈzeɾ &#124; ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ ‖ ew so mɐɾiˈɐnɐ mɐˈʃadu dɨ liʒˈboɐ] | Very nice to meet you, João. I'm Mariana Machado from Lisbon. |
| João | Muito prazer, dona Mariana. Como está? | [ˈmũj̃tu pɾɐˈzeɾ &#124; ˈdonɐ mɐɾiˈɐnɐ ‖ ˈkomu ɨʃˈta] | Very nice to meet you, Mariana. How are you? |
| Mariana | Muito bem, obrigada. Mas não posso conversar mais consigo. | [ˈmũj̃tu bɐ̃j̃ &#124; ɔbɾiˈɡadɐ ‖ mɐʒ nɐ̃w̃ ˈpɔsu kõvɨɾˈsaɾ majʃ kõˈsiɡu] | Very well, thank you. But I can't talk with you any longer. |
| João | Porque não? | [ˈpuɾkɨ nɐ̃w̃] | Why not? |
| Mariana | Porque me vou embora. | [ˈpuɾkɨ mɨ vo ẽˈbɔɾɐ] | Because I'm leaving. |
| João | Vai-se embora quando? | [ˈvajsɨ ẽˈbɔɾɐ ˈkwɐ̃du] | When are you leaving? |
| Mariana | Agora. | [ɐˈɡɔɾɐ] | Now. |
| João | Bem, então. Adeus, dona Mariana. | [bɐ̃j̃ &#124; ẽˈtɐ̃w̃ ‖ ɐˈdewʃ &#124; ˈdonɐ mɐɾiˈɐnɐ] | Well, then. Goodbye, Mariana. |
| Mariana | Adeus, João. | [ɐˈdewʃ &#124; ʒuˈɐ̃w̃] | Goodbye, João. |

## Concepts

**Fast and slow.** *Depressa* (<Lat>[dɨˈpɾɛsɐ]</Lat> - fast, quickly) and *devagar* (<Lat>[dɨvɐˈɡaɾ]</Lat> - slowly): *Mais depressa que eu.* (<Lat>[majʒ dɨˈpɾɛsɐ kɨ ew]</Lat> - Faster than me.), *Mais devagar, se faz favor.* (<Lat>[majʒ dɨvɐˈɡaɾ &#124; sɨ faʃ fɐˈvoɾ]</Lat> - More slowly, please.), *Ele fala depressa demais.* (<Lat>[ˈelɨ ˈfalɐ dɨˈpɾɛsɐ dɨˈmajʃ]</Lat> - He speaks too fast.)

**This.** *Este* / *esta* (<Lat>[ˈeʃtɨ] / [ˈɛʃtɐ]</Lat> - this) agree with the noun: *este homem* (<Lat>[ˈeʃtɨ ˈɔmɐ̃j̃]</Lat> - this man), *este mês* (<Lat>[ˈeʃtɨ meʃ]</Lat> - this month), *esta semana* (<Lat>[ˈɛʃtɐ sɨˈmɐnɐ]</Lat> - this week). *Pessoa* is always feminine, even for a man: *esta pessoa* (<Lat>[ˈɛʃtɐ pɨˈsoɐ]</Lat> - this person).

**True, I think so.** *É verdade.* (<Lat>[ɛ vɨɾˈdadɨ]</Lat> - It's true.), *Não é verdade.* (<Lat>[nɐ̃w̃ ɛ vɨɾˈdadɨ]</Lat> - It's not true.), *Acho que sim.* (<Lat>[ˈaʃu kɨ sĩ]</Lat> - I think so.), *Acho que não.* (<Lat>[ˈaʃu kɨ nɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - I don't think so.)

**To stay.** *Ficar* (<Lat>[fiˈkaɾ]</Lat> - to stay), which you met for the location of buildings, also means staying somewhere for a while: *Ele vai ficar em Lisboa.* (<Lat>[ˈelɨ vaj fiˈkaɾ ɐ̃j̃ liʒˈboɐ]</Lat> - He's going to stay in Lisbon.), *Vai ficar lá dois meses.* (<Lat>[vaj fiˈkaɾ la dojʒ ˈmezɨʃ]</Lat> - He's going to stay there two months.), *Ela vai ficar no Algarve.* (<Lat>[ˈɛlɐ vaj fiˈkaɾ nu aɫˈɡaɾvɨ]</Lat> - She's going to stay in the Algarve.)

**With each other.** *Um com o outro* (<Lat>[ũ kõ u ˈotɾu]</Lat> - with each other): *Falamos um com o outro em inglês.* (<Lat>[fɐˈlɐmuz ũ kõ u ˈotɾu ɐ̃j̃ ĩˈɡleʃ]</Lat> - We speak English with each other.)

**At one o'clock.** One o'clock is singular: *à uma da tarde* (<Lat>[a ˈumɐ dɐ ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - at one in the afternoon), against *às duas*, *às três*.

**Why not?** In Portugal *porque* is written as one word, also in the question *Porque não?* (<Lat>[ˈpuɾkɨ nɐ̃w̃]</Lat> - Why not?). The spelling *por que* is Brazilian.

## Exercises

1. Introduce yourself as John Smith from New York.
2. Ask someone to speak more slowly, please.
3. Say that he speaks too fast.
4. Say *I think so* and *I don't think so*.
5. Say that she's going to stay in Lisbon for two months.

::: details Answers
1. Sou o John Smith, de Nova Iorque.
2. Mais devagar, se faz favor.
3. Ele fala depressa demais.
4. Acho que sim. / Acho que não.
5. Ela vai ficar em Lisboa dois meses.
:::

| Mariana | Igualmente, João! Adeus e tudo de bom! | [iɡwaɫˈmẽtɨ &#124; ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ ‖ ɐˈdewz i ˈtudu dɨ bõ] | Likewise, João! Goodbye and all the best! |

**Introducing yourself.** Starting with *eu sou* sounds emphatic, and Portugal puts the article before first names: *Muito prazer, sou o João, venho de Nova Iorque.* (<Lat>[ˈmũj̃tu pɾɐˈzeɾ &#124; so u ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ &#124; ˈvɐɲu dɨ ˈnɔvɐ ˈjɔɾkɨ]</Lat> - nice to meet you, I'm João, I'm from New York.) or *Chamo-me João Smith.* (<Lat>[ˈʃɐmumɨ ʒuˈɐ̃w̃ smiθ]</Lat> - my name is João Smith.)

**Leaving politely.** *Não posso conversar mais consigo* sounds stiff, almost rude. People soften the exit with an apology: *Olhe, peço imensa desculpa, mas agora tenho mesmo de ir andando!* (<Lat>[ˈɔʎɨ &#124; ˈpɛsu iˈmẽsɐ dɨʃˈkuɫpɐ &#124; mɐz ɐˈɡɔɾɐ ˈtɐɲu ˈmeʒmu dɨ iɾ ɐ̃ˈdɐ̃du]</Lat> - I'm so sorry, but I really have to get going!), *Gostava imenso de ficar à conversa, mas tenho mesmo de me pôr a andar, senão chego tarde!* (<Lat>[ɡuʃˈtavɐ iˈmẽsu dɨ fiˈkaɾ a kõˈvɛɾsɐ &#124; mɐʃ ˈtɐɲu ˈmeʒmu dɨ mɨ poɾ ɐ ɐ̃ˈdaɾ &#124; sɨˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈʃeɡu ˈtaɾdɨ]</Lat> - I'd love to stay and chat, but I really have to get going or I'll be late!)

**Give a reason.** *Porque me vou embora* on its own sounds odd. People mention the actual reason: *Porque tenho o tempo contado.* (<Lat>[ˈpuɾkɨ ˈtɐɲu u ˈtẽpu kõˈtadu]</Lat> - because I'm short of time.), *Porque tenho de apanhar o metro.* (<Lat>[ˈpuɾkɨ ˈtɐɲu dɨ ɐpɐˈɲaɾ u ˈmɛtɾu]</Lat> - because I have to catch the metro.)

**Letting someone go.** The natural answer to someone in a hurry is not a dry *adeus* but *Ah, então não a prendo mais!* (<Lat>[a &#124; ẽˈtɐ̃w̃ nɐ̃w̃ ɐ ˈpɾẽdu majʃ]</Lat> - oh, then I won't keep you!, to a woman) or *Fica para uma próxima, com mais calma!* (<Lat>[ˈfikɐ ˈpɐɾɐ ˈumɐ ˈpɾɔsimɐ &#124; kõ majʃ ˈkaɫmɐ]</Lat> - another time, when there's less of a rush!)

**Slang.** *Pôr-se a andar* (<Lat>[ˈpoɾsɨ ɐ ɐ̃ˈdaɾ]</Lat> - to get going, to leave); *pirar-se* (<Lat>[piˈɾaɾsɨ]</Lat> - to clear off, very informal): *Tenho de me pirar!* (<Lat>[ˈtɐɲu dɨ mɨ piˈɾaɾ]</Lat> - I've got to run!); *falar a correr* (<Lat>[fɐˈlaɾ ɐ kuˈʁeɾ]</Lat> - to speak very fast, literally "running").
